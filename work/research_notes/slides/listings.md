## 2. Lay explanation (for a 15-year-old)
CARFAX Listings is a website and app, like a giant online car lot, where car dealers pay a monthly fee to show the used cars they want to sell. What makes it different from CarGurus is that every car comes with a free CARFAX report showing whether it has crashed, how many owners it has had and whether it was serviced.
- **Example 1:** You search "used Honda Civic near me" on carfax.com. You see 200 Civics from local dealers, and each one has a free report attached. The dealers paid CARFAX a subscription to appear there; you pay nothing.
- **Example 2:** You filter for "1 owner, no accidents". Only CARFAX can run that filter on its own data, because it owns the history records.
- **Example 3:** A dealer pays extra for "Showroom" (launched 2026), and its cars appear higher in your search, like a sponsored post.
- **CarGurus** does the same job (dealers pay to list, shoppers search for free). It is famous for labelling each car "Great Deal" to "Overpriced", and it has been doing this longer and at bigger scale.

## 3. How it makes money
- **Dealer subscription, priced per location per month** [D]: "Segment revenue is primarily driven by the number of dealer locations enrolled in dealer subscription products (Advantage, Car Listings, and CARFAX For Life), the average monthly price per location on each product…" ([10/A p.340](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3603533&documentType=report&eventId=695345&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=340)).
- Product definition [D, 10/A p.263]: "Car Listings : A dealer subscription product that lets dealers post vehicles for sale on CARFAX's high - traffic website and apps . Every vehicle listing includes a free CARFAX report , history - based value and verified reviews ."
- **Flat subscription, no pay-per-lead** (2021 view): "We didn't do any charge per lead. It was just, you sign up, you subscribe and it will tell you each month how many leads you're getting and all that." [X, #9, ex-CARFAX Director Strategic Insights, 10 Feb 2021, p.11]
- **Premium tier**: Showroom is a paid add-on: "We launched product and listings. That's new. It's an add-on." (Calderone, GS, [627.8s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4152674&documentType=transcript&eventId=725748&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=627.8)) [IR]
- **List prices: not disclosed.** One bundle data point: an AutoNation luxury store pays under $5,000/month for Advantage + Listings + For Life combined [X, #6, per X_expert_calls.md].
- **CarGurus model (comparison)** [3P, 10-K p.6]: "We derive our revenue from (i) dealer subscription fees, (ii) advertising from auto manufacturers and other brand advertisers, and (iii) partnerships with financing services companies." Listings are tiered packages "priced monthly, quarterly, …" (10-K p.8).

## 4. Customers and usage
- **Listings subscriber count: not disclosed.** CARFAX has "over 40,000 dealer customers" across all products [D, 10/A p.262]. "The average dealer has between one and a half and two products" (Calderone, Q2-26) [IR].
- **Traffic** [D, 10/A p.263]: "approximately 23 million monthly average unique visitors to the CARFAX U.S. website and apps" (FY25). This is all of carfax.com, including report readers, not Listings only.
- **54m visits/month**, "#1 most visited automotive website (US)" per Semrush, Feb 2026 ([slide 65](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3352878&documentType=slide&eventId=676565&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&sp=65)) [D-deck].
- **Competing "#1" claims:** CarGurus calls itself "the No. 1 visited automotive shopping site in the U.S." and footnotes that it measures against "CARFAX.com Listings (defined as CARFAX.com Total Visits minus Vehicle History Reports)" (Similarweb, Q2-26) ([CarGurus Q2-26 8-K p.3](https://web.quartr.com/companies/6015?companyId=6015&documentId=3671663&documentType=report&eventId=662781&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=3)) [3P]. In other words, CARFAX is #1 counting report traffic, while CarGurus is #1 once report traffic is stripped out. Neither company discloses the Listings-only figure for CARFAX.
- **One-store lead data** [X, #6, AutoNation JLR store GM, 30 Sep 2026]: "This month alone, it would have been 78 CARFAX listings, 60 CarGurus." (p.10). YTD close rates: "It's 4.29, CARFAX 1.98, CarGurus 4.54, Cars.com 3.55, Edmunds 1.88, and TrueCar 1.08." (p.11; the first figure is Autotrader).

## 6. Moat in plain words
**What CARFAX has:** a brand everyone asks for, and history data no one else has, so it can put a free report on every car and offer filters ("one owner") that rivals cannot. The evidence:
- 96% in-market awareness [D, 10/A p.263]; "Two million times a month, consumers come in or go online and ask the dealer for a CARFAX report." (Fredericks, Investor Day, [5936.45s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3659313&documentType=transcript&eventId=676565&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=5936.45)) [IR]
- "We are the new kid on the block in actually both those areas, even though we have the strongest recognized consumer brand and data that nobody else has." (Eager, GS, [988.65s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4152674&documentType=transcript&eventId=725748&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=988.65)) [IR]

**What it does not have (yet):** the marketplace network effect, meaning the most shoppers and dealers in one place, plus SEO leadership. Here CarGurus leads:
- "CarGurus has been the gold standard of SEO for a long time" [X, #2, ex-Cox AVP Product, 9 Jul 2026, p.3]
- "we could eventually out-SEO CarGurus. I don't think it's happened yet." [X, #9, 10 Feb 2021, p.11]
- **Verdict (Inference):** in Listings the moat protects the *report*, not the *marketplace*. CARFAX is a challenger with <10% share after 12 years.

## 7. Fit with the rest of Mobility Global
- **Same data, same brand:** each listing carries a free VHR and a history-based value. Advantage "unlocks the data, not just for the vehicle history report, but the data that would power Listings or would power the Service Loyalty product." (Eager, GS, 988.65s) [IR]
- **Traffic engine:** report-seekers become shoppers. The 10/A funds brand ads "to raise unaided awareness, drive more direct traffic and high - quality leads to Car Listings" [D, p.271].
- **Cross-sell:** "Most dealers are only on 1.5 products. We know that we can sell three or four or five products to those dealers." (Fredericks, Investor Day, [6197.77s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3659313&documentType=transcript&eventId=676565&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=6197.77)) [IR]. New Homegrown (dealer-sold, dealer-serviced cars) sits inside Advantage and surfaces on the consumer side [IR, Q2-26].
- **Channel conflict (the cost of fit):** Listings made the marketplaces rivals, and they moved to AutoCheck: "That's why a lot of them pulled the plug and moved to AutoCheck when CARFAX stood up their used car listings." [X, #3, ex-CARFAX BIG sales manager, 3 Jul 2026, p.8]. Management's response: "We kind of look at that situation as one where carfax.com and the dealer's websites benefit from having the best product in the marketplace." (Eager, Q2-26, [3694.24s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4111696&documentType=transcript&eventId=704668&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=3694.24)) [IR]

## 10. Risks (with quotes)
- **Lead quality / low conversion:** "It's the highest volume of leads, but it's one of the lowest, if not the lowest, closing percentage of leads." [X, #6, p.11]. And: "it might just be redundant leads that we're getting from other sites because now people are going to check the CARFAX on the listing page." [X, #6, p.12]
- **First to be cut:** the GM would drop Listings and For Life before Advantage [X, #6, per Segments doc §2(d)].
- **Scale and talent gap vs CarGurus:** "I think CarGurus has probably invested more and they probably have more automotive talent pushing their product where they understand the dealers better." [X, #5, ex-CARFAX Director Data Acquisition, 18 Jun 2026, p.15]
- **Channel conflict feeding AutoCheck:** marketplaces moved to AutoCheck once CARFAX became a rival [X, #3, p.8]; partners "could shift over time, as we, or they, develop products and solutions that compete with each other" [D, 10/A p.302].
- **Monetisation vs consumer trust caps yield:** "You will see things on other listing sites that you do not see on ours. We are testing our way into a lot of those opportunities to make sure that we do not see a drop in consumer experience." (Eager, GS, 1178.28s) [IR]
- **Traffic cost and AI/search disintermediation:** "…competing products from internet search engine providers, such as those that provide dealer and vehicle pricing and other information directly in search results or decreases in customer use of search engines, for example, as a result of the continued development of AI technology, could also adversely impact traffic to our websites." [D, 10/A p.299]. Group advertising expense was $134m / $152m / $176m in FY23 / 24 / 25 [D, 10/A p.450 via D1]. CarGurus' own risk list includes "AI-generated search engines" [3P].
- **Bundle go-to-market misfire:** "Our sales incentive was incenting our reps to wait until they were both ready and to sign them up on the package. Hindsight's 20/20." (Eager, GS, [913.53s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4152674&documentType=transcript&eventId=725748&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=913.53)) [IR]
- **Short contracts:** "most of the providers have a 90-day out" [X, #6, p.13]; marketplaces sell on "30-day out" terms [X, #7, p.3].
