# Mobility Global — Segments and Products
## Evidence from the antitrust record and expert calls

*MBGL research engagement · 26 October 2026*

## Bottom line

Mobility Global is two businesses with different economics. **CARFAX** (65.3% of FY25 revenue; Adj. EBITDA margin 46.9%) grew 8–12% a year in FY24–6M-26, and the 10-Q says that growth is "primarily driven by price increases". **B2B** (34.7%; margin 32.4%) grows 3–6%, mostly from new business [D/C]. The primary documents and the experts agree on what CARFAX sells: a dealer-paid report that consumers ask for by name. The experts add four things the filings do not say. Dealer penetration is close to complete. List price increases of "up to 5%-8%" fall mainly on smaller dealers, and one large-group store has been flat for two years. AutoCheck is 20–67% cheaper. And the marketplaces that once showed CARFAX exclusively, the conduct challenged and cleared in Maxon v. CARFAX (2013–18), have moved to AutoCheck since CARFAX became their listings rival. The courts treated CARFAX's lead as consumer preference, not contractual foreclosure. The 2026 evidence still fits that view for Advantage, but not for Listings: at the one dealer that measured it, Listings brings the most leads and converts them at 1.98%. The thinnest evidence is on B2B Strategy & Planning, International and AI, where no expert speaks with first-hand authority.

### How the money is made

**CARFAX dealer products** are monthly subscriptions per location. The filing names the drivers:

> *“Segment revenue is primarily driven by the number of dealer locations enrolled in dealer subscription products (Advantage, Car Listings, and CARFAX For Life), the average monthly price per location on each product, and the number of BIG customers and their average monthly price per customer.”*  
> — [D, Form 10/A (Quartr 3603533) p.340]

**BIG** sells data fields to lenders and insurers on one-year contracts with monthly minimums, billed per VIN [X, #3]; the filing's phrase is "BIG minimums with usage tiers" [D, 10/A p.341]. **Consumer reports** are pay-per-report and transactional.

**B2B** is 82–83% subscription: OEM and dealer-group enterprise agreements, dealer-rooftop APIs (Market Scan) and planning datasets, plus transactional campaigns, VIN pulls and Recall outreach [D, 10/A p.341].

**Price versus volume.** The only quantified split in any filing:

> *“Revenue increased $29 million in the three months ended June 30, 2026 as compared to the three months ended June 30, 2025, and increased $64 million in the six months ended June 30, 2026 as compared to the six months ended June 30, 2025, primarily attributable to an increase in subscription revenue, driven primarily by price increases of approximately $16 million and $36 million, respectively, and continued new business growth of approximately $8 million and $20 million, respectively.”*  
> — [D, 10-Q Q2-2026 (Quartr 3699447) p.27]

Price was therefore about 56% of 6M-26 revenue growth ($36m of $64m) [C]. Customer types [D-deck, slide 10]: dealers 62%, OEMs & suppliers 20%, financial/consumer/other 18%. The 10/A gives dealers ~60% and OEMs ~10% [D, 10/A p.299].

**Inference:** CARFAX carries the group. In 6M-26 it earned about three-quarters of segment Adj. EBITDA before Corporate [C], and its growth is mostly price on a saturated dealer base. B2B grows on volume but at lower margins, and its FY25 Adj. EBITDA fell while revenue rose [C].

### (d) CARFAX Listings and marketplace relationships

Listings is ~22% of CARFAX revenue (~$251m [D-deck/C]). It is the product that turned the marketplaces from distribution partners into competitors.

**What the documents say** 

**The antitrust record.** The only primary court-related texts are the 10-K litigation notes:

> *“The complaint alleges, among other things, that, in violation of antitrust laws, CARFAX entered into exclusive arrangements regarding the sale of CARFAX vehicle history reports with certain auto manufacturers and owners of two websites providing classified listings of used autos and light trucks.”*  
> — [D, IHS Markit FY2016 10-K (Quartr 223426), Note: Litigation p.80]

> *“On October 25, 2013, the plaintiffs served a second amended complaint with similar allegations purporting to name approximately 469 auto dealers as plaintiffs, and counsel for plaintiffs indicated that there may be additional claimants.”*  
> — [D, IHS Markit FY2016 10-K (Quartr 223426), Note: Litigation p.80]

> *“On September 30, 2016, the District Court granted CARFAX's motion for summary judgment, dismissing all claims in the complaint. The plaintiffs filed their notice of appeal on October 28, 2016.”*  
> — [D, IHS Markit FY2016 10-K (Quartr 223426), Note: Litigation p.80]

> *“The court has stayed the case pending the outcome of the appeal of the first case described above.”*  
> — [D, IHS Markit FY2017 10-K (Quartr 223425), Note: Litigation p.79]

**Court history, as reported in secondary coverage (court opinion not retrieved):**

- **Case.** Maxon Hyundai Mazda et al. v. CARFAX, Inc., S.D.N.Y. No. 1:13-cv-02680-AJN (Judge Alison J. Nathan). About 120 dealers filed in April 2013, seeking $50m+ trebled. The second amended complaint (Oct 2013) named ~469 dealers [D, 10-K].
- **Allegations (Sherman Act §§1–2).** Exclusive deals with Autotrader.com, Cars.com and OEM CPO programs foreclosed AutoCheck. The complaint alleged that CARFAX held ~90% share and AutoCheck ~10%, that the website deals foreclosed 75% of franchised-dealer used vehicles (Autotrader alone 53% of independent-dealer vehicles), and that CPO cars were 36% of reports sold. These are allegations, not findings.
- **29 Sep 2014.** The motion to dismiss was largely denied. The parties then agreed that summary judgment on foreclosure alone would decide the case.
- **30 Sep 2016.** Summary judgment for CARFAX [D, 10-K]. As reported by CARFAX's economic consultant: the CPO agreements were short-term or terminable; AutoCheck could reach consumers through other channels; and the plaintiffs' foreclosure estimate was unreliable.
- **1 Jun 2018.** The Second Circuit affirmed by summary order (No. 16-3717-cv, 726 F. App'x 66). It held that plaintiffs bear the burden of showing foreclosure.
- **13 Jan 2017.** A follow-on suit was stayed pending the appeal [D, FY2017 10-K]. Its final disposition was not found, and no FTC or DOJ action was found. Autotrader (Sep 2013) and Cars.com (Jan 2014) had opened to AutoCheck while the suit was pending.
