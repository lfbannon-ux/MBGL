# AI / moat evidence: predecessor era (IHS Markit FY2021, S&P Global 2020-11 to 2026-09)

Scope: 65 IHS Markit / S&P Global transcripts (SPGI event 620148, the Mobility Global Investor Day, excluded as covered elsewhere). Entries are in chronological order. Quotes and Quartr links are re-used from the verified sweep files in `/home/user/MBGL/work/transcripts/predecessor_mentions/` (cited per event as `src:`), except where marked "re-read", where I read the full transcript again with the moat/AI lens (IHS Markit Q2 FY21 [8567], Q3 FY21 [5801], S&P Investor Day 2022 [44944], Q1 2025 spin call [314678], Investor Day 2025 [349214]) and, for one passage, Stifel 2024 [199854].

Tier legend: [IR] = management statement; [X] = analyst assertion. Where an analyst question is the only content, the question is summarised and tagged [X].

Coverage note: tags follow the AI_SPEC list; I added three working tags: FIT (how management justifies Mobility's place in S&P), PRODUCT (non-AI product innovation relevant to differentiation) and INDUSTRY-RISK (management acknowledging end-market change).

Headline finding up front: across these 65 events management never describes an AI threat to CARFAX/Mobility, no analyst asks one on a Mobility-specific basis in the record, and management's explicit "AI + proprietary data" framing at S&P Global (Investor Day 2025) is made for the four post-spin divisions only. The one explicit moat claim ("very strong competitive moat") appears in the spin announcement call, April 2025.

## (d) Changes in tone vs earlier events and how the narrative evolved, 2020 to 2026
1. 2020-21 (merger, IHS Markit as owner). Moat language is operational: "must-have", "critical", renewals strong, products help dealers sell and acquire cars (Tavernier, Uggla). The strategic data risk is on the supply side: connected-car/VIN data ownership is an open question for two to three years (Jun 2021). No AI vocabulary. S&P's early view (Nov 2020) treats Transportation as "really more of a mobility business", a growth asset whose data can flow into Market Intelligence.
2. 2022 (integration). Moat framing shifts to embeddedness and breadth: "play to our advantage because we have data analytics and research products that we can serve all of the users", "inherent hedge", stickiness from supply-chain/EV/autonomy disruption. Investor Day 2022 is the high-water mark of detailed moat evidence: 130,000 sources, 67 million forecast lines, 45,000 dealers, 28 million Car Care consumers, North American "most differentiated assets", and the explicit concession that rivals could replicate with time and effort. Still no AI in Mobility's own segment, and analysts push on growth quantum (7-9% vs ~10% history).
3. 2023 (first AI mentions). Retention dips ("declined modestly"), then "stabilized" (Jul). AI appears for the first time: Autopilot internal LLM tool (Jul), Mobility data on the Marketplace as an "early opportunity to integrate new AI capabilities" (Nov). Management rebuts a "saturated CARFAX" view (Sep). AI is treated as an efficiency and distribution opportunity, never as a threat.
4. 2024 (portfolio-fit questions). Moat claim sharpens in the context of fit: "fairly unique ... not another publicly traded company" (May), "hard to recreate" (Jun), MarketScan as the missing price-paid dataset. Analysts begin to challenge fit (Bernstein, May: "not obviously aligned"; Peterson concedes different end customers, Jun). GenAI appears as a brand feature (Car Fox, Oct) and as benefit from S&P's AI/data-extraction tools (Jun). Tone on the sector: "going through a lot of change" (Feb).
5. 2025 (spin decision). The moat vocabulary is at its strongest at the announcement ("very strong competitive moat", "deeply entrenched in the workflows") while the rationale shifts from competitive position to portfolio/customer fit: "similar characteristics, similar customer profiles ... greater ability to share technological and data resources" among the four core divisions, and dealers as the largest Mobility customer set that S&P cannot cross-sell into (May). The moat is asserted as a feature of the asset that stays with Mobility.
6. 2025-26 (execution and post-spin). Mobility language narrows to "mission-critical" and "essential" nature of the products and subscription resilience; after completion S&P describes what it lost as "higher revenue growth ... modestly lower margins" (Jul 2026), and its own identity as benchmarks, proprietary data and AI.
