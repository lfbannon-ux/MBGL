## Event summary
- Transportation segment (CARFAX, automotiveMastermind, Polk-type automotive insights, Maritime and Trade) grew 15% organically in Q3, helped by easy COVID-era comps; recurring +17%, non-recurring +8%. FY outlook 14%-16% organic, with underlying growth in the high single digits.
- Transportation adjusted EBITDA margin was 48.1%, down 280 bps FX adjusted, as 2020 benefited from COVID cost actions.
- CARFAX for Life is described as an established dealer service-loyalty product; CARFAX consumer and dealer transactions were strong.
- Supply-chain/chip shortage is a temporary headwind to new-car OEM/dealer marketing products (automotiveMastermind bookings, digital marketing); used-car and service markets are neutral to positive. Management says it is not material and does not change the outlook.
- Management credits the automotiveMastermind acquisition and enterprise rollout (two major dealers) as a key forward component, and cites automotive as supporting the company at the top end of the 5%-7% long-term range for 2022.
- Context: the call was the last as IHS Markit ahead of the S&P Global merger; no separation or spin discussion.

### 8. Lance Uggla, Chairman and CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Kevin McVeigh, Credit Suisse): how much of the supply chain-driven strength is structural versus a near-term shock?
- Quote: "As our automotive business is expecting as car sales wane through the supply chain shortages that are unprecedented, coupled with less used cars available. Those can have temporary negative impacts."
- Detail: Uggla says most supply chain effects are short-term, positive for chemicals pricing, economic and country risk, and consulting; but the automotive business faces temporary negatives from falling car sales and fewer used cars available. Net across the firm, short-term pluses and minuses leave guidance unchanged from the start of the year.
