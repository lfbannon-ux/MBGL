# SYNTHESIS — Mobility Global (MBGL): one-page investment synthesis

Prepared 2026-10-06. It synthesizes D1–D12, QUALITATIVE, K_session_tables, TARGETS_TIMELINE and accounting_notes.

**Tiers:** [D] disclosed · [C] computed · [E] estimate · [IR] management verbal · [X] third party · [context] general knowledge.

## What the business is
MBGL was spun from S&P Global on 2026-07-01 [D]. It is a VIN-keyed automotive data utility with two segments:
- **CARFAX** (65% of revenue): dealer vehicle-history subscriptions, Listings, Car Care, and the lender/insurer group BIG. The FY25 segment Adj. EBITDA margin was ~47% [C].
- **B2B** (35% of revenue): automotiveMastermind, Polk audiences and recall, Market Scan, and planning forecasts. The FY25 margin was ~32% [C].

FY25 revenue was $1,750m: 81% subscription, ~60% from dealers, ~10% from OEMs, and 17% international [D] (QUALITATIVE §1–3). The model is B2B2C: the consumer generates demand, while the dealer pays [D] (10A p.380; D6 §1).

## The moat in one sentence
MBGL owns the only VIN-level history record that a dealer can hand to a consumer. It is built from ~177k give-get sources and 38bn+ records that grow ~9% a year [D/C], and it is backed by a brand consumers ask for by name and by 36 OEM certified pre-owned (CPO) mandates. That makes it a wide moat in CARFAX; B2B's moat is only moderate, since low-cost substitutes exist (D1 §1, §5).

**Non-comparability caveats**
- **The IHS era (P0) is broader.** It includes Maritime & Trade, and Jane's until 2019. Automotive was ~80% → >90% of the segment. It uses a Nov FYE and an IHS Adj. EBITDA definition that excluded aM performance compensation. The 47.6% FY21 margin therefore **cannot** be read as "margin fell to 40%".
- **FY22 is a ~10-month SPGI period.**
- **Slide 91 is labelled "NOT prepared on a consistent basis".**
- **The carve-out includes ~$100m of S&P allocations** [C] (D3 §2).
- **MBGL Adj. EBITDA is +0.7–1.8pt above SPGI adj. OP** on the same business [C] (D4 §12).
- **The Q2-26 definition shrank from 10 to 6 exclusions** (accounting_notes; D4 §1).

**Inference.** On a like-for-like view, growth has stepped down from ~10% (2015–22) to ~8.5% (2023–25) to ~7% (2026). Margins have been flat at ~40% on the generous measure.

## Five most important findings
1. **Growth is now mostly price, and new business is fading.**
   - Price was ≈4.2pt of 7.4% 6M-26 growth [D/C]. New business fell from ~$12m (Q1) to ~$8m (Q2) [C].
   - The Form 10/A had called the same Q1 growth "new business" (D7 §5; D4 §3).
   - Pricing power is real, but it depends on dealer profitability (D9 §4).
2. **The CARFAX moat is wide and still widening. B2B's is not.**
   - Growth beat industry units in 10 of 11 years, by +10pt a year on average [C].
   - Sources grew +6.5% a year and records +9.3% a year [C].
   - The CARFAX margin rose to ~48% [C].
   - B2B grew only +3% / +6% and had −18% FY25 flow-through [C] (D1, D2).
3. **The margin narrative keeps resetting.**
   - The SPGI 41–43% target was missed by 110–310bp while revenue was on plan [D/C].
   - The standalone cost and one-time costs both drifted to the top of their ranges.
   - Expansion has been pushed to 2028, and SBC rises to 3–4% of revenue [IR] (D10, D3, D4 §7–9).
4. **The standalone capital structure changes the earnings picture, not the cash picture.**
   - Interest goes from $13m to ~$110m [C], and gross leverage is 2.7x [IR].
   - The "25% of normalized NI" dividend is actually 34–44% of reproducible normalized NI.
   - It is still only ~19% of FCF [C] (D3 §5).
5. **Disclosure retreated just as metrics weakened, and incentives are loosely tied to performance.**
   - The retreats: NRR pulled, organic constant-currency growth no longer printed, business lines dropped, the segment measure switched in the quarter B2B OP fell to $3m [D] (D4 §0).
   - The incentives: PSUs converted to time-vested awards, no TSR metric, unlimited committee discretion, insiders hold <1% [D] (D5).

## Key red flags
- **The FY26 guide was "reaffirmed" on May-12** while Q1 constant-currency growth (~7.4% [C]) was already below its 7.5% floor. It was cut 3 months later (D10 §3).
- **The two filings describe the same growth differently.** The 10/A says "new business", the 10-Q says "price" (D4 §3).
- **Transaction costs were booked in B2B, and the segment measure switched in the same quarter** (D4 §2).
- **Units changed when the dollar figure would have breached the range.** The standalone cost moved from "$20–25m" to "150bp" [C] (D4 §8).
- **The Adj. EBITDA definition gained an "and other" catch-all**, while the Q2 deck still prints the old definition (D4 §1).
- **Ad expense grew faster than revenue** (+31% vs +18%), and CAC is rising [D/C] (D1 §6).

## Open questions
- Enrolled CARFAX locations, gross retention and price per location: the drivers the 10/A names but never prints (D12 Q1).
- A dollar bridge from FY25 $711m to the FY27 base on the current definition; whether SBC is in the pay metric (D12 Q2).
- The share of traffic coming from search and AI; any AI licensing (D12 Q3).
- Data-acquisition cost growth; Europe's losses and break-even (D12 Q8, Q10).
- Why the IHS-era Transportation margin (47.6%) sits above the carve-out (~40%). Scope, allocations and definitions explain some of the gap; the rest is unquantified.
- 2008–09 resilience: there is no data (D1 §4.2).

## Honest counters
- **The moat evidence is strong and largely audited** (revenue, margins, source and record counts). Subscription revenue held even in 2020 (+3% recurring [D]), and downturns produced price relief, not churn (D9 §3).
- **Revenue guidance has mostly been met.** The FY26 cut is ~1pt like-for-like (D10).
- **Several flags cut against the company's own optics**: the Q2-25 recast lowers growth, and the 10-Q volunteered the price split (D4).
- **The B2B dip may be a one-year investment cycle.** 6M-26 flow-through recovered to ~50% [C].
- **Capex is ~1% of revenue, there is no capitalized-software asset, and FCF is ~$365m standalone [C].** The balance sheet supports the capital return without strain.
- **The bear cluster in D11 is a de-rating risk, not an impairment risk.** It applies only if the market prices MBGL as a 9–10% compounder.
