# D12 — Management Q&A: Mobility Global (MBGL)

Prepared 2026-10-06 by the Part D synthesis analyst. It follows `analysis/D_SPEC.md`. **Synthesis only. No new research was done.**

**Tiers:** [D] disclosed · [C] computed · [E] estimate · [IR] management verbal · [X] third party · [context] general knowledge.

**Selection logic.** The three primary questions target the three load-bearing assumptions of any long thesis:
1. Core growth is durable. It is now mostly price (D7).
2. Margins expand from a reset base. The predecessor missed a target, and the base keeps moving (D3, D4, D10).
3. The consumer-brand leg of the moat survives AI and search. Ad intensity is rising (D1, D6).

Each primary question is built so that a numerical answer either confirms or refutes the bear case in D11.

## Primary question 1: price, volume and retention

**Question (verbatim as asked):**
> "Your 10-Q attributes about $36 million of first-half growth to price and about $20 million to new business, and new business slowed from roughly $12 million in Q1 to $8 million in Q2. Your Form 10 names enrolled dealer locations and average monthly price per location as the CARFAX revenue drivers. Can you tell us how enrolled CARFAX dealer locations changed in 2025 and the first half of 2026, what gross revenue retention was, and what percentage of dealers took a price increase at their last renewal? And if those are 'great numbers', why not publish them, since NRR was pulled from the Investor Day deck?"

**Why it matters**
- Price accounts for ≈4.2pt of 7.4% 6M-26 growth; new business is ≈2.3pt [D/C] (D7 §5; 10Q p.27).
- The Form 10/A attributed $26m of Q1-26 to "new business". The 10-Q implies ~$12m [C] (D4 §3).
- The 10/A names locations and price per location as the drivers, but quantifies neither [D] (10A p.340; D2 (a)).
- NRR was "pulled" and described as "meaningfully north of 100%" [IR] (D4 §10).
- **This is bear case #1 in D11.** If the location count is flat or falling, the 5–7% core is a pure pricing algorithm on a fixed base, with a ceiling set by dealer profitability.

**Good answer:** gives numbers or tight ranges.
- Locations up low single digits, gross retention in the mid-90s, and price taken on a defined share of the base, with a stated renewal cadence.
- Reconciles the 10/A "new business" vocabulary with the 10-Q "price" split.
- Commits to printing a location or retention KPI in the FY27 guide or the 10-K.

**Evasive answer:**
- "We're not a software company."
- "It's value, not price."
- "NRR is meaningfully above 100%" with no number.
- Pivoting to the 10–15x dealer ROI study.
- "We don't manage the business on those metrics", which contradicts the 10/A's own driver definition.

**Listen for:**
- Whether "new business" includes upsell at renewal. If it does, the 10/A and 10-Q splits are definitional, not a re-attribution.
- Any mention of dealer gross-profit pressure, or of concessions on price-increase requests.
- Whether CARFAX price increases differ between franchise and independent dealers.
- Hesitation to commit to a disclosure date.

## Primary question 2: the margin bridge and its credibility

**Question (verbatim as asked):**
> "S&P Global set a 41–43% adjusted operating margin target for Mobility for 2025–26 and delivered 39.9% while revenue grew on plan. Since May the standalone cost has moved to the high end of $20–25 million, one-time costs to $75–110 million with half now capitalised, and the start of 50 basis points of annual expansion to 2028. Can you give us a dollar bridge from FY25's $711 million Adjusted EBITDA, on the old ten-item definition, to your FY27 base on today's six-item definition: the standalone run-rate, the S&P allocations you are replacing, the segment reallocation you flagged, and what goes into 'severance and other'? And will SBC at 3–4% of revenue be excluded from the metric your executives are paid on?"

**Why it matters**
- The SPGI target was missed by 110–310bp [D/C] (D10 §1; D4 §9).
- The MBGL Adj. EBITDA measure is +0.7 to +1.8pt above SPGI's adjusted OP on the same business [C] (D4 §12).
- The definition shrank from 10 to 6 exclusions and now includes an "and other" catch-all [D] (D4 §1).
- The standalone increment of 150bp ≈ $26m [C] exceeds the top of the original $20–25m range (D3 §2).
- One-time costs: $78m was expensed pre-spin, and the overlap with the ~$100m guide is unstated [D] (D4 §7).
- SBC rises by ~$34–53m a year [C] (D5 §4).
- **This is bear cases #6 and #10 in D11.**

**Good answer:**
- A bridge with dollars for allocations replaced (~$100m [C]), the new standalone cost, reallocations between segments and corporate, and stand-up capex.
- A statement on whether FY25's $711m changes on the new definition.
- A cap or a named list for the "other" add-back.
- An incentive design that charges SBC, or that uses FCF or EPS alongside Adj. EBITDA.

**Evasive answer:**
- "We've been in the 40% range for three years." That uses a different measure from the SPGI target.
- "The SPGI target was set by prior management."
- "We'll give more at the FY27 guide."
- Restating "~50bp a year" without a base year.

**Listen for:**
- Whether management concedes that the ~39.1% reset base is on a more generous measure than the 41–43% SPGI target.
- Any recurring "other" items planned for 2027, such as integration, AI re-platforming or rebranding.
- How much of the ~$50m capitalised stand-up spend is software that will be amortised. This affects future GAAP earnings and FCF optics.

## Primary question 3: consumer traffic, ad intensity and AI

**Question (verbatim as asked):**
> "Advertising expense grew 31% from 2023 to 2025 against revenue up 18%, and your Form 10 says customer-acquisition costs are rising as ad platforms dial up monetisation and that search and AI are threats to traffic. What share of CARFAX consumer sessions and VHR views now arrive via search or AI answer engines, how has cost per unique visitor trended over three years, and do you license history data to any AI or answer-engine provider today? At what ad-to-revenue ratio would you conclude the brand is getting more expensive to defend rather than stronger?"

**Why it matters**
- Ad expense went from $134m to $176m, which is 9.0% → 10.1% of revenue [D/C] (D1 §6; D2 (a)).
- The 10/A says CAC is rising [D] (10A p.343), and names search and AI as risks to traffic [D] (10A p.297–299).
- IP protection against AI use of the data is uncertain [D] (10A p.298).
- The S&P cross-licence is non-exclusive [D] (D1 §6; D8 §2).
- The moat's consumer leg (the dealer pays for consumer trust, D6 §1) is the only leg where the record shows rising cost.
- **This is bear case #3 in D11.**

**Good answer:**
- Traffic-mix numbers, with stable or falling cost per visitor or per VHR view.
- A clear position on licensing: a paid API or licensing terms for AI agents, with "infrastructure to monetize those agents" [IR] (D7 §3) made concrete.
- An explanation of the FY23–25 ad step-up as a Car Care user acquisition (46m → 53m+ [IR/D]) with a stated payback.

**Evasive answer:**
- "Our data is behind a firewall."
- "AI is an accelerant for us."
- "We're the #1 visited auto site."
- Talking about AI only as internal productivity (99.5% decoding accuracy, 4–5x engineering productivity) rather than about consumer distribution.

**Listen for:**
- Whether ad spend is described as brand defence or as growth investment, and whether it has a ceiling.
- Any slippage in "Show me the CARFAX" or MUV trends.
- Whether OEMs or marketplaces are starting to display history without the CARFAX brand.
- Any mention of answer-engine partnerships.

## Honest counters

- **Management has said repeatedly that it will not give P×Q or retention KPIs** ("We're not a software company" [IR]). Q1 may get the same answer however it is phrased. In that case the answer is itself the data point.
- **Several questions assume a red flag that may be definitional.** The new-business vs price discrepancy and the B2B cost booking (D4 Honest counters) may both be definitional. A good answer could close them quickly.
- **The FY27 guide, the first 10-K and the first DEF 14A (~Apr-2027) will answer Q2, Q6 and Q7 regardless.** Meeting time might be better spent on Q1 and Q3, which no scheduled disclosure will resolve.
- **The AI question may be premature.** No traffic loss is visible in the record. The ad increase coincides with Car Care growth to 53m+ users, which may be a deliberate investment rather than a defensive cost.
