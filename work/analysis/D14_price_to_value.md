# D14: Price-to-value and remaining pricing power, by segment (2026-10-26)

**Question:** how much of the value it creates does Mobility Global charge for, and how much pricing power is left?

**Method:**
- **Price:** taken from expert calls [X], filings [D] and management [IR].
- **Value:** taken from the only customer-side value anchors on record: an expert's recollection of a CARFAX study, management's ROI claims, and dealer volumes.
- **Ratios:** computed by me [C]; assumptions are labelled [E].
- **Two kinds of headroom:**
  - *Economic headroom:* value ÷ price.
  - *Practical headroom:* how much more customers will accept before they switch, cut or escalate. The practical limit is the one that binds.

## 1. CARFAX dealer VHR: the core of the question

**Price per car** [C]:
- Calculation: one store "sold about 150 cars a month" and pays "$1,000 a month", unlimited [X #4]. That is **≈$6.7 per car sold**, or ≈$13 per used car if half the sales are used [E].
- Cross-check: Advantage revenue per dealer is ≈$1.4k a month [C, D2], and ≈$1.29 of Advantage revenue per report view [C, D2].
- Against the car: **≈0.02–0.05% of a $28,377 used car** [C]. Management makes the same point: "The cost of the CARFAX report as a percent of that continues to decline." (Fredericks, Investor Day 2026-05-12) [IR]

**Value per car:**
- From the expert anchor: "They were selling cars, it was two to three days faster, and for about $200-$300 more." [X #1, recalling a CARFAX study, no date]
  - That is $200–300 a car, before the value of selling 2–3 days sooner (≈$60–150 at an assumed $30–50 a day holding cost [E]).
  - On these figures **price is ≈2–7% of value; the dealer keeps 93–98%** [C].
- From management: "This translates into 10 to 15x ROI." (Fredericks, Investor Day) [IR]. That puts price at **7–10% of value**.

**But practical headroom is much smaller than economic headroom:**
- **2021 baseline:** "The dealers could pay twice what they're paying and would still be getting a great deal on the CARFAX vehicle history report product." [X #9, former CARFAX director, Feb 2021]
- **2026 ceiling estimate:** "I think we're another 12%-15%. I think that's when the cost gets pretty high." [X #1, Jul 2026]. Also: "They are going to need a new value add before they can continue to raise prices so aggressively." [X #1]
- **Switching trigger:** an OEM would switch only after a breach or "a 25% price increase" [X #4].
- **Signs the ceiling is near in parts of the base:**
  - The AutoNation store's price has been flat for 2+ years on a group deal [X #6].
  - Increases are 4–5% for large groups and ≈8% for small stores, and small stores are the ones that cancel [X #1].
- **Where price is still rising fastest: usage.** Heavy users have been re-tiered, e.g. an appraisal-heavy dealer from ≈$2,500 to $4,800 to ≈$6,800 a month [X #2]: "We can't be giving away 10,000 CARFAX reports a month for $2,000." [X #2, his inference]
- **The alternative caps the price:** AutoCheck costs 20–67% less [X #1, #3, #4, #6]. A Cox alumnus says that if Experian offered the same data at half price: "Fine, I don't really care where it comes from." [X #2]

**What is left in money terms** [C, Inference]:
- 12–15% of ≈$434m Advantage revenue is **≈$52–65m** of cumulative price uplift, mostly flowing to EBITDA.
- At the 2026 pace (price ≈$36m in 6M-26 across the company [D, 10-Q p.27]) that runs out in **≈2–4 years** unless new value-adds reset the ceiling. Management's pattern is to launch something before each increase: "CARFAX has always been, before they do a price increase, they will typically launch something new to justify it." [X #1]

**Inference:** CARFAX captures a small share of the value it creates, typical of an essential, low-ticket input. Pricing power is limited by three things, not by value:
- dealers' cost scrutiny ("Just use AI to do it all" [X #2]);
- a credible half-price substitute;
- group procurement.

The 2021 view of "2x" headroom has narrowed to "12–15%" in five years. That is consistent with D7's finding that 2026 growth is mostly price.

## 2. Other CARFAX products

- **Consumer reports: weak.**
  - The $44.99 single report sits against free dealer-provided reports and $9.99 resellers.
  - "The typical scenario is the dealer makes it available at no charge online and in their showroom" (Fredericks, Investor Day) [IR], and "The consumer group's been shrinking a lot" [X #3].
  - Consumer value is real, but dealers capture it on their behalf.
- **OEM CPO: strong per contract, little revenue.** The mandate is absolute ("You cannot certify a car without that"; Nissan runs it twice on every CPO car), but the OEM pays only "Couple thousand a quarter" for a ≈280-dealer region [X #4]. The power shows up indirectly, by forcing dealer adoption.
- **Listings: priced at or above value.**
  - In 2021 management deliberately under-priced it with intro offers [X #9].
  - In 2026 it is the #1 source by lead volume at #6's store but closes at 1.98%, about half the major marketplaces' 3.6–4.5% [X #6].
  - The GM would cut it first. Listings is ≈22% of CARFAX revenue [C, D2], and **no price lever** is visible.
- **Service Loyalty / For Life: unproven value.**
  - Management claims +19pt service retention and +16pt OEM loyalty [IR], but attach is under half of franchise Advantage dealers [IR].
  - One GM: "The truth is I don't really know if it's added much in terms of business with that." [X #6]
- **BIG: two markets.**
  - **Insurance:** odometer and history data feed underwriting. Prices are cents per VIN and increases are accelerating, "3–5% every ~18 months, insurance moving to 5–8%" [X #1/#3]. The constraint is onboarding speed ("two to three years" plus state approvals [X #1]), not price resistance.
  - **Lending:** ≈90% of lenders pull history, split ~50/50 with AutoCheck, and subprime lenders buy both [X #1, #3]. This is commodity pricing.

## 3. B2B

- **Price data are thin.** No price point is disclosed, and no expert quotes a B2B price.
- **Marketing & Sales:**
  - Management's value claims are high: aM "14x ROI", 56k+ users, 650k attributed sales [D-deck]; Data Studio close rates +40% [IR]. On the 14x claim, price would be ≈7% of value.
  - **The P&L shows no pricing power:** FY25 revenue +6% with Adj. EBITDA −3% and an incremental margin of −18% [C, Segment P&L]; non-subscription revenue fell 16% and 4% [D].
  - **Inference:** either the ROI claims don't hold for the marginal customer, or competition and OEM budget pressure cap price.
  - Also, the sales silos mean products are rarely sold together ("I don't think those discussions ever took place" [X #5]), which limits bundle pricing.
- **Strategy & Planning: two different products.**
  - **Polk registration data:** users know of no alternative, J.D. Power PIN is the nearest, and Polk is the "de facto pathway" despite a ≈90-day lag [X #2, #4]. Likely moderate-high pricing power, unquantified.
  - **Forecasting / FAST:** management concedes "a low-cost alternative" and that "the only real alternative is a spreadsheet" [IR]. AI shrinks exactly that gap, so pricing power here is low.

## 4. Bottom line

- **CARFAX dealer VHR:** price is ≈2–10% of the value it creates [C]. The binding constraint is practical: ≈12–15% more over 3–4 years [X], then it needs new value-adds. That is ≈$50–65m of cumulative uplift [C].
- **Elsewhere in CARFAX, pricing power is weak:** consumer, Listings and For Life have no lever. The exception is insurance, which has headroom but is slow to monetise.
- **B2B:**
  - Registration data has power that is hidden but plausible.
  - Marketing & Sales and forecasting show no pricing power in the numbers, whatever the ROI claims.
- **Usage-based re-tiering (AI and automated pulls) is the new lever.** It can raise price without list increases, but it is also the most likely trigger for a move to a half-price AutoCheck [X #2].

## Honest counters

- **Thin value anchors:** the $200–300 / 2–3-days-faster figure is an ex-employee recalling a company study [X #1], and the 10–15x ROI is management's own [IR]. Neither is independent, and neither isolates CARFAX's value *over AutoCheck*. That incremental value is what limits price.
- **Narrow sources:** the 12–15% ceiling is one person's judgement [X #1/#3, same expert], and the flat-price evidence is one luxury store on a group contract [X #6].
- **The headroom can reset:** management has consistently created new value-adds before raising prices, which moved the ceiling before. Future reliability, Data Studio and agents could do it again.
- **B2B evidence is mostly absent:** the B2B conclusions rest on the P&L, not on price data.
