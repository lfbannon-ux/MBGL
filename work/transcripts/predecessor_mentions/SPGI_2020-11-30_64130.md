## Event summary
- The call announces the all-stock S&P Global / IHS Markit merger; the IHS Markit Transportation segment (which housed the automotive/Mobility business) is mentioned only as one of the pillars of the combined company. No segment numbers, no CARFAX, Polk or aM are named.
- Peterson lists "transportation and engineering" among the core market segments of the pro forma company, and Uggla lists "transportation" among IHS Markit's focus segments.
- The only substantive commentary is Peterson's answer to an analyst asking about shedding non-core assets: he calls Transportation "very powerful," high growth, institutional, and "really more of a mobility business," and sees opportunity to push its data into Market Intelligence. He explicitly did not signal a divestiture.
- In the closing Q&A, Peterson names "automotive" data among sets to be moved quickly into the Market Intelligence platform after close.
- Deal context: 0.2838 S&P shares per IHS Markit share (about 67.75/32.25 ownership), about $480M cost and $350M revenue synergies, 6.5%-8% pro forma organic growth.

### 4. Q&A - Doug Peterson, President and CEO, S&P Global - Alex Kramm (UBS) asks whether the combined company could shed non-core assets and become leaner again given S&P's divestiture history.
- Quote: "It's an institutional business. It's actually really more of a mobility business when you think about what the future is of transportation."
- Detail: Peterson says all IHS Markit businesses fit: indices with S&P indices, Financial Services with Market Intelligence, Energy and Resources with Platts, and some benefit to Ratings. Anticipating that the question may target Transportation, he calls it a very powerful part of the combination, high growth and institutional, and says it is really more of a mobility business given where transportation is heading. It fits well with data and analytics already inside the business, and he sees opportunity to leverage some of that content in the Market Intelligence platform. He ties it to energy transition: EVs affect the whole energy chain (metals used in batteries, battery technology), and S&P can bring learning there, helped by the 451 Research acquisition. He did not propose any divestiture; he emphasized strong fit across all verticals. Uggla adds (next turn) only about the AWS data lake, Kensho and data science, nothing Transportation-specific.

### 5. Q&A - Doug Peterson, President and CEO, S&P Global - Ashish Sabadra (Deutsche Bank) asks how cloud lowers the risk of merging large data assets and how the Market Intelligence platform will roll out for IHS Markit product sets.
- Quote: "We think that we will be able to quickly move information like OTC market pricing and data, upstream oil and gas, automotive, private market data, supply chain and trade data, etc., into the Market Intelligence platform, as well as linking our marketplace in the Data Lake."
- Detail: Preceding turns: no platforms need merging, everything is additive; Market Intelligence has 350,000 active users and is close to final rollout; both companies began data-center-to-cloud plans about three years ago with mostly the same vendors, making a cloud-to-cloud integration easier. In this turn, Peterson lists automotive among the IHS Markit data sets (with OTC pricing, upstream oil and gas, private market data, supply chain and trade) that can quickly be moved onto the Market Intelligence platform and linked with the marketplace and Data Lake; work starts immediately and he says they will move as fast as possible at close. No automotive-specific metrics.
