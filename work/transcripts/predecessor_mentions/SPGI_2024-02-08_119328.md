## Event summary
- Mobility Q4 2023 revenue +9%: Dealer +14% (fourth straight double-digit quarter; price realization, new-store growth especially CARFAX for Life, plus MarketScan acquisition), Manufacturing +2% (planning and marketing solutions), Financials and other +5%. Adjusted expenses +10%; operating profit +8%; margin down 30 bps in the quarter and 20 bps to 38.8% for FY2023.
- Mobility cost drivers: benefits alignment for heritage IHS Markit staff, higher commissions on OEM and dealer outperformance, and inorganic cost from MarketScan.
- 2024 guidance for Mobility: revenue +8.5% to +10%, adjusted margin 39%-40% (up from 6.5%-8.5% guided for 2023), with investment in new products.
- Portfolio actions in 2023 included divestiture of the aftersales business in the Mobility division (alongside Engineering Solutions); management says it keeps reviewing the portfolio for tuck-ins and possible divestitures but names no Mobility separation.
- Management flags the automotive segment as "going through a lot of change" when building conservative 2024 guidance.

### 2. Ewout Steenbergen, EVP and CFO - Prepared
- Quote: "As we go through the divisions, you will see these factors impacting expenses and margins this quarter, particularly in Market Intelligence and Mobility."
- Detail: Q4 total expenses grew about 2% (revenue +7%); compensation was elevated by salaries (hiring and inflation) and by higher benefit costs from finalization of the benefits realignment of IHS Markit employees. He flags that these factors weighed on Market Intelligence and Mobility expenses and margins, producing slightly higher expense growth than expected. (In Q&A he later calls the benefits realignment a one-time step-up that stays in the baseline from 2024.)

### 6. Doug Peterson, President and CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Jeff Silber, BMO): what are you looking for in portfolio optimization (tuck-ins or divestitures) and could there be a large divestiture?
- Quote: "We also had a couple of small divestitures."
- Detail: Peterson did not name Mobility. He said S&P applies discipline to the portfolio on top-line growth, margin expansion and fit; in 2023 it shut down about 8,000 sub-scale indices and small products across Commodity Insights and Market Intelligence and made a couple of small divestitures. He cited themes of private markets, sustainability, supply chain analytics, and risk and credit, and said the company weighs shareholder interests and technology leverage. In the prepared remarks (about 390s) he said that with integration complete, S&P will keep reviewing the portfolio through tuck-in acquisitions or potentially further divestitures. Included as a portfolio/separation reference; no Mobility-specific plan stated.

### 7. Doug Peterson, President and CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Craig Huber): where might guidance be conservative, on cost or on the top line, and which segments could come in ahead?
- Quote: "We know that the automotive segment is going through a lot of change."
- Detail: Peterson says GDP growth is the number one driver; guidance assumes a soft landing and builds in some conservatism around central-bank rate cuts and geopolitics, with upside if rates fall faster or economies grow faster. At the segment level, he flags that the automotive segment is going through a lot of change and that the energy transition is changing the commodity cycle, both considered in setting the guidance.
