## Event summary
- Cheung explains the rationale for the announced Mobility spin: a deep vertical SaaS business serving the whole auto value chain (suppliers, OEMs, dealers, consumers), about 70% tied to the used car market, with dealers the largest customer group; it cannot be cross-sold S&P products, so it is best as a standalone company.
- Mobility financial profile cited: 8.4% growth last year and 39% margin.
- Disynergies are expected to be very limited and minimal; data shared with other S&P units can be handled via arm's-length agreements.
- Timing: the spin will take about 12-18 months; it is intended to be a 100% tax-free spin; more disclosures are promised through the year.
- The standalone company would have more freedom to invest and pursue organic and inorganic growth. Capital return guideline stays at 85%, and OSTTRA proceeds (about $1.4B) go to buybacks.

### 2. Martina Cheung, President and CEO - Q&A (two turns)
- Question: Why spin Mobility off now, and what is the strategic rationale?
- Quote: "The Mobility business we view as a deep vertical SaaS-based business serves the entire automotive value chain all the way from suppliers to OEM to dealerships and to the end consumer. About 70% of the business is directly related to the used car market."
- Detail: She has thought about Mobility for some time and personally loves the business, citing a leadership team that has grown it compellingly: 8.4% growth last year and a 39% margin, with consistently high innovation. The rationale rests on the end customer: a vertical SaaS business across the automotive value chain, about 70% used-car related, where dealerships are the largest customer revenue base and also the largest number of customers. S&P would not go to a dealer to sell the other S&P products, so unlocking enterprise value from Mobility is hard. Synergies existed and were delivered, but the business is best served as a standalone company with strong products, leadership, customer base and growth/margin profile and multiple growth paths.

### 3. Martina Cheung, President and CEO - Q&A (two turns)
- Question: What is the impact of the Mobility spin on rest of S&P: synergies, shared data platforms, margin profiles?
- Quote: "We have said that we think disynergies would be very limited, very minimal. Practically speaking, it's going to take about 12- 18 months."
- Detail: Disynergies are expected to be very limited. Parts of Mobility already operate independently and are less integrated than something like Ratings. Data that Mobility provides to other S&P groups can be continued through arm's-length agreements during the spin process. The separation takes about 12-18 months and the team is moving fast through the first phase. More disclosures will be shared with investors over the course of the year.

### 4. Martina Cheung, President and CEO - Q&A (two turns)
- Question: What constraints has Mobility had under S&P governance and what operational advantages would it have standalone?
- Quote: "It is a group of leaders who can turn investment dollars very quickly around opportunity and runway for additional growth. I do think that when you look at that business and think again about the composition of the end customers, their ability to grow both organically and different pathways, whether it is organic, inorganic, et cetera, we think will be much better served as a standalone business."
- Detail: Mobility is highly innovative and good at converting investments into fast-growing products. Recent investments are only beginning to show benefits. Standalone, it could grow and invest by its own distinct strategy through organic or inorganic paths, giving a compelling financial profile and value for shareholders. No new numbers.

### 5. Martina Cheung, President and CEO - Q&A (capital allocation)
- Question: Moderator, noting OSTTRA being sold and Mobility separation pending, asks about optimal capital structure and allocation priorities.
- Quote: "From a mobility perspective, we have also said that we would expect that to be a 100% tax-free spin."
- Detail: S&P remains committed to its 85% capital return guideline. OSTTRA is expected to yield about $1.4 billion, previously stated to go to share repurchases. The Mobility spin is expected to be 100% tax-free, which she flags as important for shareholders. S&P will remain disciplined owners of capital; the new CFO (Eric) is bringing transparency on capital allocation and organic investment performance by division, with an evolution rather than revolution in strategy.
