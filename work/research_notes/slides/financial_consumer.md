## 2. Lay explanation (for a 15-year-old)
CARFAX has the "life story" of almost every car: crashes, owners, mileage, oil changes. Apart from car dealers, three other kinds of customer pay for that story.
- **Insurers:** you apply for car insurance and say you drive 8,000 miles a year. The insurer pays CARFAX a few cents to check the car's past odometer readings and accidents, then sets your price.
- **Lenders:** a bank lending you $25,000 for a used car pays CARFAX to check the car wasn't flooded, isn't salvage and hasn't had its odometer wound back. If you stop paying, the bank can use service records to see where the car is being serviced.
- **You, buying from a neighbour:** no dealer is there to give you a free report, so you buy one on carfax.com.
- **Bonus (Car Care / For Life):** the free Car Care app reminds you about oil changes. Dealers pay CARFAX (For Life) so that the reminder sends you back to *their* service bay. The shop then sends CARFAX the service record, which makes the database better.

## 3. How it makes money
- **BIG: per-VIN data with minimums.** The filing calls it "BIG minimums with usage tiers" ([10/A p.341](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3603533&documentType=report&eventId=695345&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=341)) [D], counted as subscription. Revenue driver: "the number of BIG customers and their average monthly price per customer" [D, 10/A p.340].
  - Expert detail [X, #3, p.3]: "They're going to charge, they say it's per VIN, but it's basically anytime they provide data, they're going to charge the insurance company or the lender call it $0.10 for the data."
  - "Just to give you guys an idea, insurance companies will pay anywhere from, I would say, $0.05 up to $0.25 per VIN." [X, #3, p.6]
  - Customers buy fields à la carte: "Very rarely would an insurance or a finance company want a full vehicle history report because they're four, five, six, eight pages long." [X, #5, p.7]
  - Price cadence: "It's roughly every year and a half, every 18 months or so, and then 3%-5%, but they're fairly custom." [X, #3, p.6]
- **Consumer: transactional, pay-per-report.** Not split out; it sits in CARFAX non-subscription revenue ($215m FY25, with BIG transactional revenue) [D, 10/A p.355 per Segments doc]. Price points (experts; they conflict):
  - "I think they charge, it's on their website, like $45 or $50 to go pull your report." [X, #3, p.9]
  - $29.99 [X, #4], which the Segments doc treats as likely stale.
  - The VinAudit comparison page cites a **$44.99** CARFAX report [3P, via challengers report].
  - Most consumers pay nothing: "Most consumers, though, the typical scenario is the dealer makes it available at no charge online and in their showroom because it builds that confidence." (Fredericks, Investor Day, [9483.13s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3659313&documentType=transcript&eventId=676565&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=9483.13)) [IR]
- **For Life: dealer monthly subscription per location** [D, 10/A p.340]; price not disclosed.

## 6. Moat in plain words
CARFAX holds the **odometer and service readings** that insurers need and rivals lack, collected for free from 92,000+ shops in a give-get swap. Insurers also take years to approve a new data supplier, so once CARFAX is in, it tends to stay.
- "The advantage that CARFAX has is CARFAX has all the odometer readings." [X, #1, p.9]
- "It was taking CARFAX two to three years to onboard a new insurance company from the time they said they were interested." [X, #1, p.10]. This barrier cuts both ways: it slows CARFAX's growth, but it also protects incumbency.
- Supply side: "CARFAX will typically give that away for free as long as the service shop agrees to send them all the data whenever they get somebody come into their shop." [X, #1, p.6]; 92,000+ dealers and service shops, 6,300 police agencies [D, 10/A p.263].
- **Weak spot:** in lending the moat is thin (commodity title, brand and total-loss data shared with AutoCheck) [X, #3]. In consumer reports, dealers give the report away for free.

## 10. Risks (with quotes)
- **Underwriting volume is cyclical and fading:** the filing's volume tailwind fell from $17m (FY24) to $13m (FY25) to $2m (Q1-26) [D, 10/A pp.350/355/347]. S&P earlier flagged "slowing consumer activity" in Financials (Q2-22, +3%) [IR].
- **Slow, regulated onboarding:** insurers: "they're regulated by each individual state. They have auditors and regulators. It can take years for them to essentially get a new data provider approved." [X, #3, p.4]
- **Lending is a commodity:** "Auto finance is also much more competitive with AutoCheck. It's a little more of a commodity on the auto finance side." [X, #3, p.4]
- **Consumer reports shrinking / given away:** "The consumer group's been shrinking a lot over the years" [X, #3, p.9]. Dealers give most reports free [IR].
- **For Life value contested:** "The truth is I don't really know if it's added much in terms of business with that." and "Yeah, I would probably get rid of it." [X, #6, AutoNation JLR GM, 30 Sep 2026, pp.9–10]. Attach is under half of franchise Advantage dealers [IR].
- **Bundle reversal:** "You're, in essence, getting two yeses in order to put those products in versus selling them à la carte, where you need to get to one yes." (Eager, Q2-26, [3411.03s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4111696&documentType=transcript&eventId=704668&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=3411.03)) [IR]
- **Data exclusivity is contractual:** "If such agreements are not sufficiently robust or enforceable, or if counterparties fail to honor them, we would not be able to maintain the exclusivity of our data sets and the competitiveness of our products and solutions may decrease." [D, 10/A p.301]
- **AI and cheap data:** "Third parties could also use our data with AI tools to create their own insights and potentially supplant our products and services." [D, 10/A p.298]
- **Source concentration:** almost all BIG evidence comes from one ex-employee (#1/#3) whose knowledge ends Jan 2024.
