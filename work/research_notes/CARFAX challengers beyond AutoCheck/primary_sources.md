# CARFAX / Mobility Global (MBGL) competitors and challengers: what the company's own filings and transcripts say

Scope: primary sources only. These are the Mobility Global Form 10/A (2026), the IHS / IHS Markit 10-Ks for FY2016–FY2021, and transcripts from MBGL and its predecessors (S&P Global / IHS Markit).
Tags: [D] = filing text (company claim); [IR] = management speaking on a call or at an event (company claim); [X] = analyst assertion or question; [context] = background that these sources do not support.
Quotes are verbatim and capped at two sentences. Local files were cross-checked against Quartr where noted.

## Q1. Which competitors does the Form 10/A name for each segment, and how does it describe competitive dynamics, pricing pressure, low-cost alternatives and dependence on data suppliers?

### Takeaway
The Form 10/A names four competitors, all in one sentence in Business—Competition (p.382): J.D. Power and Cox Automotive ("automotive data and analytics providers"), and Experian and Global Data ("data and information providers"). It does not split them by segment, and the Risk Factors name no company. The risk factors do describe a broad threat set:
- price competition and "lower priced competitors";
- free or cheap public data as a substitute;
- off-the-shelf AI that lowers barriers to entry;
- search engines, and competitors' SEO;
- partners that may turn into competitors;
- data suppliers that "are also our competitors," and supplier consolidation that has raised fees.

The 10/A never names AutoCheck, CarGurus, Cars.com, Autotrader or NMVTIS report providers.

### Inferences
- The four named firms read as a B2B list (Polk/registration, forecasting, valuation) plus Cox, which spans listings, valuation and dealer software. Experian is the only one that also competes directly in vehicle history reports. The 10/A names no listings marketplace, even though IHS Markit's FY2020–21 10-Ks named Cars.com, Cox and CarGurus (see Q2). That is a narrowing of disclosure in the competitor list.
- The filing does not say which "suppliers are also our competitors." [context] Plausible candidates are DMS vendors, auction and title data providers, or OEMs, but the filing names none of them.
- Exclusivity is asserted ("often on an exclusive basis") and then hedged: it is contractual, and management said "It's a mix" (Q3). Exclusivity is a partial barrier, not an absolute one.

### Gaps
- The 10/A does not split competitors by segment and gives no market-share figures.
- There is no 10/A mention of AutoCheck by product name, of NMVTIS providers, or of listings marketplaces as competitors.
- The supplier-competitors are not identified.

## Q2. What did the IHS Markit 10-Ks (FY2016–FY2021) say about the CARFAX antitrust litigation, Transportation-segment competition and CARPROOF?

### Takeaway
**Litigation.** The FY2016 and FY2017 10-Ks describe a 2013 S.D.N.Y. dealer antitrust suit. It alleged that CARFAX had exclusive VHR arrangements with certain automakers and with the owners of two used-car listings websites. The second amended complaint named about 469 dealers. CARFAX won summary judgment dismissing all claims on 30 Sep 2016; the plaintiffs appealed, and a second dealer suit (13 Jan 2017) was stayed pending that appeal. Quartr full-text search finds no mention of the matter in the FY2018–FY2021 10-Ks, and no filing found here states how the appeal ended.

**Competitors.** The 10-K competitor lists moved over time:
- FY2016–FY2018: VHR competition is "principally with Experian and various other providers approved by" NMVTIS.
- FY2019: Experian / LMC / Urban Science / Auto Alert.
- FY2020–FY2021: adds a separate listings line naming Cars.com, Cox Enterprises and CarGurus.

**CARPROOF.** Acquired on 24 Dec 2015 for about $459.2m net of cash, to take vehicle history into Canada.

### Inferences
- The antitrust theory, per the filing's summary, was exclusivity with OEMs (CPO programs) and with two listings websites. Those are the same channels management now cites as the moat: OEM CPO requirements and marketplace integrations. In the one case summarized here, the district court found no antitrust liability on those arrangements. That suggests the exclusive-distribution model survived legal challenge at least at first instance.
- The filing does not name the "two websites." [context] It would be reasonable to assume they were large US listings marketplaces, but that is not verified here.
- CARPROOF shows the company bought rather than fought a national vehicle-history competitor in Canada. Eager's "market leader in Canada" claim most likely rests on that asset. [context: CARPROOF was later rebranded CARFAX Canada; not verified in these filings]
- The 10-K competitor list moved from naming VHR rivals (Experian plus NMVTIS providers) to naming listings rivals (Cars.com, Cox, CarGurus). That fits the company's own view that the contested ground has shifted to Listings, where CARFAX is the challenger.

### Gaps
- No filing found here states the result of the appeal or how the January 2017 follow-on suit ended. [context, unverified] Public court records reportedly show the Second Circuit affirmed in 2018, but that was not checked against a primary source in this task.
- The two listings websites and the automakers in the complaint are not named.
- The FY2017 competition wording was confirmed only from the search snippet, not a full-page read.

## Q3. What do the transcripts say about named competitors, marketplaces switching to AutoCheck, Listings being "the newest of the four", "low-cost alternatives", and why challengers fail?

### Takeaway
Every competitor name in the transcripts comes from an analyst. Management never names a rival on any call; it answers with a "different competitors at each stop of the lifecycle" framing.

**Named by analysts [X]:**
- Experian AutoCheck and GlobalData/LMC (Meuler, Investor Day 2026).
- Experian in the UK (Rosenbaum, 2024).
- "Some marketplaces have switched to Experian AutoCheck" (Bhatia, Q2-26).

**Management's concessions [IR]:**
- B2B Planning faces "a low-cost alternative." LaFeir said this about the planning/forecasting side; the analyst's question was about GlobalData/LMC.
- In Listings, "Of the four players in that space, we're the newest of the four."
- On the AutoCheck/marketplace question, Eager answered only that "we see different pockets of increased competition."

**Why challengers fail, as management explains it [IR]:**
- Consumer brand pull ("Show me the CARFAX"), so a dealer will not swap in "brand Z."
- Accident and service-detail data that "nobody else can touch."
- A 177,000-source give-get network built over decades and kept secret.
- DMV and registration data that "a lot of organizations have tried" and failed to assemble.
- A longitudinal archive that "doesn't exist anymore anywhere else."
- Workflow embeddedness ("switching cost is just too high").

### Inferences
- Management has a consistent pattern. It concedes competition in general terms ("pockets," "very good competitors," "low-cost alternative"), names no rival, and offers no data that could be checked: no AutoCheck comparison metrics, no marketplace-loss figures, no churn. The moat case therefore rests on assertion plus indirect evidence: 36 OEM CPO programs, 177k sources, 28m monthly report views, and Polk's >90% of US volume.
- "Four players" in Listings lines up with the FY2020–21 10-K listings competitors: Cars.com, Cox (Autotrader) and CarGurus, plus CARFAX. Eager did not name the three; the mapping is inference. In Listings, CARFAX is the challenger, not the incumbent, at under 10% of a $3bn+ market by its own estimate.
- The AutoCheck exchange shows a structural tension. CARFAX Listings competes with the marketplaces that also distribute the VHR. An analyst [X] asserts that some marketplaces have moved to AutoCheck, and management did not dispute it. This is the clearest on-record sign of a challenger gaining distribution. Management's defence is that dealers, not marketplaces, are the main channel for reports.
- The explanations for why challengers fail map onto distinct barriers:
  - VHR: consumer brand demand at the point of sale, plus accident and service-record depth.
  - Polk: regulatory and trust barriers to DMV data, plus the longitudinal archive.
  - Across both: workflow embeddedness.
  - Nobody claims a barrier in Planning, where a "low-cost alternative" is conceded.

### Gaps
- Management never identifies which marketplaces moved to AutoCheck, or what they were worth.
- No head-to-head data versus AutoCheck was given (record counts, accident coverage, share).
- No transcript covers the 2013–2016 antitrust case. A search of the local predecessor corpus found no Mobility-related "antitrust" mention; the only hits concern the IHS Markit–S&P merger.
- Cox Automotive, J.D. Power and CarGurus are never discussed as competitors on any call in the corpus (per D13 §6 and grep), even though Cox and J.D. Power are named in the 10/A.
