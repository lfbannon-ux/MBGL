# Cheap rivals failed; platforms now test CARFAX

Yes, CARFAX has had challengers beyond Experian AutoCheck. None of them displaced it, and the only meaningful pressure today comes through AutoCheck itself. **No well-funded, US vehicle-history startup was found.** The US challengers were small resellers of government title data (NMVTIS) charging about $4–$13 a report, plus a few free or crowdsourced sites. Their funding is almost entirely undisclosed. The one large sum, **$150M in December 2020, went to Bumper's diversified people-search parent**, not to a dedicated history-report business ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201217005623/en/The-Lifetime-Value-Co.-Announces-$150-Million-Investment-Led-By-Morgan-Stanley-Expansion-Capital)).

Three things defeated these challengers:

- **Thin data.** NMVTIS carries five fraud and theft indicators and no repair history ([DOJ/BJA](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/nmvtis_understandingvhr)).
- **No dealer channel.** Dealers, OEM certified pre-owned (CPO) programs and listing sites buy CARFAX reports and give them to shoppers free.
- **A brand that became the generic term.**

The serious threats came from platforms. A dealer antitrust suit (2013–2018) attacked CARFAX's exclusive deals and lost at every stage. Since 2025, marketplaces led by Cox's Autotrader and Kelley Blue Book (KBB) have been moving to AutoCheck. Outside the US the pattern holds: whoever controls the scarce data or the transaction channel wins. IHS bought Canada's leader (CARPROOF) rather than fight it. Lithuania's carVertical is the only challenger found that grew to real scale, and it did so in fragmented European markets where CARFAX was weak.

## NMVTIS made title checks cheap but could not buy distribution

The national title database, NMVTIS, turned state title, brand and odometer data into something any approved reseller could license. The resulting sellers cluster around 2011–2012: VinAudit in 2011, VehicleHistory.com and EpicVIN in 2012 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/vinaudit); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/vehiclehistorycom)).

Price was their only lever. VinAudit charges **$9.99 a report**, which it pitches as under 25% of a **$44.99 CARFAX report** ([VinAudit](https://www.vinaudit.com/?p=10105)). VINsmart sells bulk dealer reports for as little as **$3.50** ([VINsmart](https://www.vinsmart.com/Dealer)). DC's DMV put consumer NMVTIS fees at **$8–$12.99** as of 2020 ([DC DMV](https://dmv.dc.gov/node/1572386)).

Every reseller sells the same core data, so competition among them comes down to price and search rankings. The data gap is the deeper problem. NMVTIS "does not contain certain data, such as a vehicle's repair history" ([DOJ/BJA](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/nmvtis_consumers)). EpicVIN's 2023 drive to collect maintenance records from repair shops was an open attempt to close that gap ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2023/02/27/epicvin-seeks-out-data-from-repair-shops-for-vehicle-history-reports/)). CARFAX, by contrast, says it has more than **53 million Car Care app users** and over **90,000 dealer and service-shop partners** contributing service records ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/carfax-celebrates-50-million-car-care-users-302524036.html); [About CARFAX](https://www.carfax.com/company/about)).

Distribution sealed the outcome. Dealers and listing sites hand out CARFAX reports free, which leaves cheaper sellers mainly the private-party buyer. The funding record is close to empty. No VinAudit, ClearVin, instaVIN, FaxVin, VehicleHistory.com or VINwiki rounds were found. EpicVIN's reported "no outside investment, ~$5M revenue" comes from a weak source and is unverified. Several "Bumper" funding entries on aggregator sites (an Atlanta/India startup that raised $500K and is listed as dead, and an unrelated UK repair-finance firm) are **not** Bumper.com ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/bumper)).

These rivals have not dented the incumbent. CARFAX grew revenue **8% to $312M** in Q2 2026, with 81% from subscriptions ([Mobility Global 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002090312/000209031226000014/mbgl-20260630.htm)).

## Marketplaces, not startups, were the real challenge

The main threat has been platforms choosing which report to show. Experian worked through every opening that CARFAX left:

- **CarsDirect** (2003) and **Consumer Guide** picked AutoCheck, Consumer Guide as its exclusive provider ([F&I Magazine](https://www.fi-magazine.com/news/experian-automotive-announces-partnership-with-carsdirect-com); [Auto Dealer Today](https://www.autodealertodaymagazine.com/315272/consumer-guide-selects-experians-autocheck-as-exclusive-source-of-vehicle-histor)).
- **eBay Motors** (2009) put free AutoCheck reports on all used listings under an exclusive deal ([Vehicle Remarketing](https://www.vehicleremarket.com/news/ebay-motors-launches-free-vehicle-history-reports-exclusively-through-experian39s-autocheck)).
- **Autotrader** opened to AutoCheck in **September 2013**, ending CARFAX's exclusivity there ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2013/experian-automotive-renews-agreement-with-kelley-blue-book-partners)).
- **Cars.com** followed in **January 2014** ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/newsroom/press-releases/2014/experian-automotive-partners-with-carscom-to-make-autocheck-vehicle-history-reports-accessible)).
- **CarMax** gives a free AutoCheck report on every car ([CarMax](https://www.carmax.com/articles/free-vehicle-history-report-walk-through)).

CARFAX responded with heavy brand spending and by building its own channels. "Show Me the CARFAX" (2009) was its "largest-ever marketing initiative" ([Vehicle Remarketing](https://www.vehicleremarket.com/news/carfax-launches-campaign-to-boost-used-car-sales)); **no ad-spend figure is disclosed**. It also launched CARFAX Used Car Listings around 2014 ([DealerRefresh](https://www.dealerrefresh.com/carfax-used-car-search-listing-dealers/)). That second move created the conflict behind today's shift. CARFAX now competes with the marketplaces that distribute its reports. Management calls itself "the newest of the four" listings players, at under 10% of a $3bn+ US market ([Q2-26 call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4111696&documentType=transcript&eventId=704668&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=2534.08); [Goldman Sachs, Sep 2026](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4152674&documentType=transcript&eventId=725748&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=1178.28)).

No search found any J.D. Power/NADA, Black Book or Cox-built proprietary history report. Those firms supplied valuations alongside CARFAX and AutoCheck rather than competing with them.

## Courts treated CARFAX's lead as preference, not foreclosure

In April 2013, about 120 dealers sued CARFAX in the Southern District of New York, seeking **$50M+, trebled** ([F&I Magazine](https://www.fi-magazine.com/news/121-dealers-file-50m-plus-suit-against-carfax)). They alleged that exclusive deals with Autotrader, Cars.com and most OEM CPO programs illegally shut out rivals. The complaint alleged:

- CARFAX held about **90%** of the market and AutoCheck about **10%**.
- The website deals foreclosed **75%** of franchised-dealer used cars.
- CPO vehicles accounted for **36%** of reports sold.

These were allegations, not findings ([vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/mazda-v-carfax-inc-894269742)).

The second amended complaint named about **469 dealers** ([IHS Markit FY2016 10-K](https://web.quartr.com/companies/6412?companyId=6412&documentId=223426&documentType=report&eventId=282305&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=80)). Most claims survived a motion to dismiss in September 2014. The parties then agreed that summary judgment on foreclosure alone would decide the whole case.

CARFAX won on **30 September 2016**. The court found the CPO deals short-term or terminable, and found that AutoCheck could reach consumers through other channels ([Compass Lexecon](https://www.compasslexecon.com/cases/carfax-litigation/)). The Second Circuit affirmed by summary order on **1 June 2018** (No. 16-3717-cv, 726 F. App'x 66) ([FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-2nd-circuit/1936668.html); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/news/2018/07/arnold-porter-secures-victory-for-carfax)). A copycat suit filed in January 2017 had been stayed pending the appeal ([IHS Markit FY2017 10-K](https://web.quartr.com/companies/6412?companyId=6412&documentId=223425&documentType=report&eventId=279401&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=79)). Its final disposition was not found. No FTC or DOJ action was found.

The timing matters. Autotrader and Cars.com had already opened to AutoCheck in 2013–14, during the suit. That likely weakened the foreclosure theory, although no source states the link. Per the appellate summary, some dealers preferred AutoCheck yet stayed CARFAX customers. The courts effectively said CARFAX's dominance came from buyer preference, which is a stronger moat than contracts.

## Abroad, control of scarce data or the channel decides the winner

**Canada.** CARPROOF won through institutional lock-in:

- It was the official report of the Toronto and Ontario dealer associations ([24-7 Press Release](https://www.24-7pressrelease.com/press-release/28937/new-car-dealers-associations-of-toronto-ontario-and-carproof-join-forces-to-give-consumers-peace-of-mind-when-buying-a-used-vehicle)).
- It was the sole supplier to the Manheim and ADESA auctions for US imports ([The Auto Channel](https://www2020.theautochannel.com/news/2007/05/28/049619.html)).
- OEMs used it for certified and off-lease programs.

It also used Experian's US data for imports from 2004 ([Insurance-Canada](https://insurance-canada.ca/2004/07/06/carproof-now-includes-experian-automotive-vehicle-history-data/)). IHS bought it in December 2015 ([canadianautodealer.ca](https://canadianautodealer.ca/2015/12/carproof-sold-to-ihs-for-650-million/)) and booked **$330.0m of goodwill** ([IHS FY2016 10-K](https://web.quartr.com/companies/6412?companyId=6412&documentId=223426&documentType=report&eventId=282305&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=60)). It became CARFAX Canada in November 2018 ([Newswire.ca](https://newswire.ca/news-releases/carproof-officially-rebrands-as-carfax-canada-699272701.html)). The only visible rival is VinAudit Canada (2020), a registration-data reseller ([VinAudit](https://www.vinaudit.com/?p=4977)).

**UK.** The valuable data (outstanding finance, write-offs, theft) is held by incumbents:

- **HPI**, sold to Solera for **£78.3m** ([Insurance Times](https://insurancetimes.co.uk/solera-completes-783m-acquistion-of-hpi/1376298.article)).
- **Experian**, which supplies finance data even to budget resellers such as Total Car Check ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id940049120)).

Free government MOT history since 2005 turned basic checks into a commodity ([fuel-finder.uk](https://www.fuel-finder.uk/blog/used-car-history-check-uk-guide/)). The best-funded challenger, Cazana, raised only small rounds and sold to Cazoo for **~£25m** in 2021 ([AM Online](https://am-online.com/news/acquisitions-and-deals/2021/09/02/cazoo-acquires-cazana-for-25-million)). Cazoo then sold it on for an undisclosed sum in 2023 as Cazoo collapsed ([Car Dealer](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/cazoo-sheds-used-car-data-firm-cazana-for-undisclosed-sum-as-great-sell-off-continues/280224)).

**Continental Europe** is the exception: a challenger has scaled there. carVertical raised over 15,000 ETH (**~US$20m**) in a 2018 ICO ([Startup Lithuania](https://www.startuplithuania.com/?p=2423)). Its revenue grew to **€75.8m in 2025**, up 40% ([Forbes Romania](https://www.forbes.ro/?p=502172); company-sourced). That likely exceeds CARFAX Europe's scale, though CARFAX Europe's revenue is not disclosed. Poland's autoDNA wins through dealer and marketplace embedding ([PKO BP](https://www.pkobp.pl/media/aktualnosci/produktowe/automarketpl-jako-jedyna-platforma-ogloszeniowa-w-europie-udostepni-bezplatna-historie-pojazdu-w-partnerstwie-technologicznym-z-autodna)). Data-protection rules blocked CARFAX in Germany until its **1 July 2026 launch with mobile.de** as sales partner ([carfax.eu](https://www.carfax.eu/press/carfax-launches-in-germany-more-transparency-and-safety-when-buying-a-used-car); [Autohaus](https://www.autohaus.de/nachrichten/gw-trends/fahrzeughistorie-direkt-im-haendlerbereich-mobile-de-bindet-carfax-ein-3904767)). Management calls Europe white space with "no big-scaled incumbents" ([Investor Day](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3659313&documentType=transcript&eventId=676565&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=10394.4)). carVertical's numbers suggest that is less true than it sounds.

**Australia.** The government register (PPSR) sells finance, stolen and write-off checks for a few dollars. Equifax's CarHistory (founded by Veda) layers its own vehicle data on top ([Equifax AU](https://www.equifax.com.au/node/321)). In markets like this, credit bureaus are the natural incumbents because they already hold lender finance data.

## AutoCheck's 2025–26 marketplace gains test the dealer-channel moat

The 2025–26 shift is the first challenge to hit CARFAX's distribution itself:

- **June 2025:** KBB and Autotrader began co-branding AutoCheck on all eligible listings ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kelley-blue-book-experian-team-up-to-bring-buyers-better-vehicle-history-reports-302477997.html)).
- **January 2026:** AIM Group reported that "almost all major U.S. auto marketplaces" had ended CARFAX tie-ins in favour of cheaper AutoCheck ([AIM Group](https://aimgroup.com/2026/01/28/multiple-marketplaces-end-carfax-tie-ins-with-experian-autocheck-as-replacement/)).

When an analyst raised marketplace switching, management did not dispute it. CEO Bill Eager acknowledged "pockets of increased competition" and pointed to dealers as the main channel for reports ([Q2-26 call](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4111696&documentType=transcript&eventId=704668&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=3615.03)). The Form 10/A names Experian, but neither AutoCheck nor any marketplace, as a competitor ([Form 10/A p.382](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3603533&documentType=report&eventId=695345&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=382)). Its risk factors do warn that partners "could shift" into competitors ([p.302](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=3603533&documentType=report&eventId=695345&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=302)).

Two caveats temper the alarm:

- Only Cox's move is confirmed by a primary source, and its press release says "co-brand", not "exclusive."
- Dealer subscriptions, OEM CPO programs and carfax.com still produce more than **28M report views a month** ([Form 10-12B/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002090312/000110465926066592/tm2528763d9_ex99-1.htm)).

The open question is whether shoppers who see AutoCheck on Autotrader still demand "the CARFAX." The 2018 ruling suggests they have so far.

## Conclusion

The history inverts the usual moat story. Challengers that competed on data or price never threatened CARFAX, because buyers never chose a report by price: dealers, OEMs and listing sites chose for them. The one legal attack on that distribution failed because the courts found AutoCheck could reach buyers anyway. That makes the current marketplace defections more important than any earlier challenger. CARFAX's own move into listings gave the platforms a reason to leave, and AutoCheck is the cheaper option they are leaving for.

The international record gives the playbook for both sides. Where CARFAX faced an entrenched channel owner (Canada) it paid about US$460m to buy it. Where scarce data sits with credit bureaus (UK, Australia) it has no position. Where data is fragmented (continental Europe) a cheaply funded aggregator, carVertical, built a business of about €76m in revenue first. Investors should watch two things CARFAX does not disclose: whether dealer subscription retention holds as marketplace placements move to AutoCheck, and whether carVertical-style aggregators reach US scale before CARFAX's 2026 European expansion matures.
