## Event summary
- Mobility revenue grew 10% YoY in Q1 2023 (dealer +10%, manufacturing +11%, financials and other +11%), with adjusted operating profit +14% and margin +130 bps. CARFAX new business, recall activity and planning solutions drove growth; Market Scan added about 1 point (about $3-4M).
- Offsetting the strength, Mobility retention declined modestly as the market normalizes from used-car-heavy conditions toward new cars, mainly on the dealer side where margins are under pressure.
- Full-year: Mobility revenue guidance raised (helped by Market Scan, expected to add about 150 bps of Mobility growth) while Mobility margin guidance was tuned down because Market Scan is modestly dilutive.
- Management framed the outlook as positive: demand for forecasting data, sales and marketing products among dealers and OEMs, EV-related planning products, CARFAX product launches, and data flowing to insurers, banks and lenders. Mobility also posted double-digit Vitality revenue.
- Global vehicle sales growth cited: US about 7.5%, China about 6%, Asia (incl. Japan/Korea) about 6.5%, Europe about 7.5%.

### 1. Prepared - Doug Peterson, President and CEO, S&P Global - [customer retention]
- Quote: "As expected and previously discussed, retention rates in Mobility have declined modestly as automotive inventory levels and volumes start to normalize."
- Detail: In the customer section, Peterson notes that retention was strong and stable company-wide, improving in Commodity Insights and some other products, but declining modestly in Mobility as automotive inventory and volumes normalize (this was expected and previously flagged). He also notes some lengthening of sales cycles in parts of the business, mainly financial services customers focused on margin protection. He says that in meetings with customers, including large automotive OEMs, retailers, asset managers and banks, the common theme is that they want to do more with S&P Global, and there is a large opportunity to raise awareness of the product breadth.

### 6. Q&A - Doug Peterson, President and CEO - Manav Patnaik (Barclays) asks for color and numbers on the modest decline in Mobility retention and whether it is a broader trend.
- Quote: "We're seeing a shift that away from used cars to new cars. We actually expect that there's gonna be a lot of growth in the sales of automotive products around the globe this year."
- Detail: Peterson says the retention softening reflects structural change in the mobility and transportation business. Over the past couple of years used-car sales were very large for dealers and OEMs (who struggled to meet demand), and the market is now moving back toward new cars, with strong global growth in automotive sales expected this year. The slowdown in subscriptions is concentrated among dealers, whose margins are lower and who may be changing how they work with their sales forces, which is causing some retention declines. Elsewhere in the company he sees no change to retention rates. No retention figure is quantified.

### 8. Q&A - Doug Peterson, President and CEO - Ashish Sabadra (RBC) asks why Mobility manufacturing grew 11% versus the usual 3%-4% and whether anything changed structurally.
- Quote: "All of this benefits our Mobility business because they provide the data and analytics that dealers and OEMs and suppliers need to make decisions."
- Detail: Peterson explains that supply chains were disrupted over the past couple of years (no access to chips used in automotive, even key fobs) and demand was distorted as buyers could not get cars; manufacturing is now recovering from a depressed base. He cites global vehicle sales growth: US about 7.5%, China about 6%, Asia including Japan and South Korea about 6.5%, Europe about 7.5%. Production is growing strongly, though demand is starting to ease with inflation and higher interest rates. There is strong demand for information from the Mobility team (forecasting, industry changes, day-to-day dynamics) as the market shifts from supply-constrained to more supply and shifting customer demand.
