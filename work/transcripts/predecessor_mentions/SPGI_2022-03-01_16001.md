## Event summary

- Call held the day after the S&P Global / IHS Markit merger closed. IHS Markit's Transportation division is renamed Mobility and becomes one of six reporting divisions (Market Intelligence, Ratings, Commodity Insights, Mobility, Indices, Engineering Solutions).
- 2022 Mobility guidance: high-single-digit revenue growth with margins in the low 40s percent.
- Drivers: dealer interest in used-car and service offerings; OEM/manufacturing demand for forecast products amid supply-chain uncertainty.
- Mobility is cited as a cross-division revenue-synergy source (expertise into ESG/energy transition) and a Mobility tile (global vehicle registrations, 150+ countries) was added to the S&P marketplace on day one.
- A Mobility/EV information asset is cited as an input to the combined ESG and energy-transition product suite.
- No CARFAX, Polk or automotiveMastermind names are used on this call.

## Entries

### 1. Division naming
- Speaker/section: Doug Peterson, President and CEO, S&P Global; Prepared
- Quote: "IHS Markit's Transportation division will be reported as the Mobility division going forward."
- Detail: Peterson describes the post-merger reporting structure: IHS Markit's Transportation division becomes the Mobility division, while Indices combines relevant products of both companies and IHS Markit's Engineering and Product Design business (inside the Consolidated Markets and Solutions division) becomes Engineering Solutions. The aim is to preserve each franchise's strength while combining capabilities. He adds that pro forma recurring revenue would have been 76% of the combined company's 2021 revenue, a six-point favorable mix shift versus S&P Global standalone.

### 3. Mobility demand drivers (dealer and manufacturer)
- Speaker/section: Ewout Steenbergen, EVP and CFO; Prepared
- Quote: "In the dealer market, we see strong interest in our used car and service offerings, while on the manufacturing side, high levels of uncertainty relating to the supply chain are driving demand for our forecast offerings."
- Detail: Continuing the Mobility outlook, Steenbergen says supply-chain bottlenecks and inventory challenges will likely persist through 2022, but the company expects to raise market penetration of core products. Dealers show strong interest in used-car and service offerings; automakers, facing supply-chain uncertainty, are pulling demand for forecasting products. The same paragraph moves on to Indices (mid-to-high single-digit growth, high-60s margins, ESG index investment limiting margin upside) and Engineering Solutions (low-single-digit growth, mid-teens margin).

### 6. Mobility EV information for ESG/energy transition
- Speaker/section: Doug Peterson, President and CEO; Q&A (Ashish Sabadra, RBC, asked how the deal improves competitive position in ESG and energy transition)
- Quote: "There's information about electric vehicles in the Mobility business, and then there's data products and Market Intelligence."
- Detail: Peterson says S&P has invested in ESG for eight or nine years and formed its Sustainable1 organization to accelerate growth. IHS Markit adds energy-transition and ESG products: energy products from its ENR business (now in Commodity Insights), electric-vehicle information in Mobility, and data products in Market Intelligence. Together these create a stronger ESG suite under a three-pronged strategy (internal strategy, building a high-growth business, engagement with global standard-setting), plus the company's own sustainability goals. ESG is called one of the most exciting growth areas. (Sabadra's prior comment referenced a $600 million revenue run-rate by 2025 for ESG/energy transition.)
