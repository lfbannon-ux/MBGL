## 1. Bottom line

1. **Only one hard quantification of price exists anywhere in the record.** The 10-Q says revenue growth was "driven primarily by price increases of approximately $16 million and $36 million" (Q2-26 and 6M-26), against "continued new business growth of approximately $8 million and $20 million" [D] (10Q p.27).
   - Price ≈ **55–56% of revenue growth** [C] (16/29; 36/64).
   - That is ≈ **3.6 pt** (Q2) and **4.2 pt** (6M) of growth [C] (16/439; 36/859).
   - New business adds only **1.8 pt / 2.3 pt** [C].
2. **CARFAX is the price engine.** CARFAX subscription growth "was primarily driven by price increases". B2B growth was "primarily due to continued new business growth" [D] (10Q p.28–29).
3. **Doctrine is value-based, not escalator-based.** The CFO rejects the "3%, 4%, 5%" annual-increase framing. He still says the 5–7% core growth is "the majority … in value" [IR].
4. **Disclosure history.** S&P Global named price as the Dealer growth driver every quarter of 2023. It then stopped and said "new business growth" from Q1-24. The Form 10/A used "improved contract terms". The 10-Q is the first MBGL filing to say "price increases". See D4 §3 for the Q1-26 attribution conflict.
5. **Not quantified anywhere:**
   - B2B price escalators;
   - consumer report pricing;
   - AI/agent monetisation;
   - price per dealer location, which the 10A itself names as the CARFAX revenue driver;
   - price vs volume within "new business".

### Pricing mechanics disclosed [D] (10A p.341)

- **Subscription:** "monthly, annual or multi-year subscriptions". Examples are CARFAX dealer products, "BIG minimums with usage tiers" and the "Market Scan API priced by dealer rooftops".
- **Transactional:** "CARFAX consumer pay-per-report purchases", recall outreach that is "volume-based", and Planning one-time deliveries.
- **Billing:** generally invoiced annually in advance (10A p.447).

## 5. Analysis

**Price share of growth, 1H-26 [C]**

| | Q1-26 (implied, 6M − Q2) | Q2-26 | 6M-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue Δ, $m | 35 [C] (455 − 420) | 29 [D] | 64 [D] |
| Price, $m | ~20 [C] | ~16 [D] | ~36 [D] |
| New business, $m | ~12 [C] | ~8 [D] | ~20 [D] |
| Other (underwriting, non-subscription, FX), $m | ~3 [C] | ~5 [C] | ~8 [C] |
| Price as pt of growth | ~4.8 [C] | ~3.6 [C] | ~4.2 [C] |

Q1 = 6M − Q2. The Form 10/A gave a different Q1 split: new business $26m and underwriting $2m (D4 §3).

**Inferences**
- **Price is the larger share and new business is fading.** New business fell from an implied ~$12m in Q1 to ~$8m in Q2 [C]. That matches management's account of a stalled bundle sales motion. Q2 price (~$16m) was below the implied Q1 (~$20m) [C], consistent with "pricing actions take effect early in the year". 2H growth therefore leans on new business recovering, with less price help.
- **The algorithm is consistent with price, but the volume leg is not visible.** The "5–7% core, majority value" algorithm [IR] matches ~4 pt price + ~2 pt new business [C]. However, the volume leg is reported only in dollars of "new business"; there is no rooftop count, churn or ARPU. A price-led model with ~40,000 dealers that is "highly pinned" in franchise [IR] has finite headroom. Management's ROI claim (10–15x [IR], from its own 2023 Economic Model study [D] ID-deck notes) is the only evidence of that headroom, and it is not independent.
- **SPGI-era "new business growth" probably included price** (2024–25). If so, the 2024–25 Dealer growth of ~10–11% [IR] was not as volume-driven as presented. This is unproven and rests on the vocabulary shift and the D4 §3 arithmetic.

## Honest counters

- **"Price increases" may include mix and upsell.** It can include customers moving to higher tiers or adding products at renewal, which is value-based and not a pure list-price increase. The 10-Q does not define it, so "~4 pt of price" may overstate pure like-for-like price.
- **Evidence for pricing power.**
  - The CARFAX brand ("Show me the CARFAX", ~2m requests/month [IR]).
  - 36 OEM CPO programmes requiring the report [IR].
  - A report cost that is falling relative to the vehicle price.
  - These make price increases at ~5% of CARFAX revenue [E] plausible and repeatable rather than one-off.
- **The SPGI vocabulary shift may be stylistic.** Different CFOs (Steenbergen, then Craig and Aboaf) wrote different scripts, and SPGI never quantified Mobility price, so "price dropped from the script" is weak evidence by itself.
- **B2B is the opposite case.** B2B growth is new-business led and the Planning competitor is described as low-cost. Pricing risk there is competition, not over-pricing. A low B2B price contribution is not a red flag in itself.
- **The bundle reversal was a packaging/go-to-market problem, not price resistance**, per management. Lengthened cycles were caused by two decision-makers [IR]. No dealer churn from price is reported.
