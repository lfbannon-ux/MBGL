## 2. Cross-call findings (2025–26 calls)

### Demand and penetration
- **Saturated.** >90% of dealers and ~95% of large groups use CARFAX [X, #1]. "The slowdown isn't because the model doesn't work, it's because they've run out of customers." [X, #4]
  - #4 also says "I don't know how they're growing higher single digits." Inference: price, BIG and Listings are invisible from his OEM seat, so this is no evidence against the reported growth.
- **Consumer pull.** 98% of consumers would name CARFAX for a history report [X, #2]. 40–50% of shoppers ask for it in person, and the rest have already seen it online [X, #6]. Shoppers visit ~1.2 dealers [X, #2].
- **Dealer trials.** Cars sold 2–3 days faster and for $200–300 more (Sonic and AutoNation, 7–10 years ago) [X, #1]. #3 gives the same figures for Lithia.
- **Scale.**
  - CARFAX revenue is ~$1bn [X, #1, #3]. Dealers are 70–75% of it [X, #5].
  - #4 gives ~50,000 locations and "10,000+" pings a day. Inference: that is implausibly low against 28m+ dealer-shown reports a month [IR] (D1 §2).

### Pricing

**Dealer list increases**
- They run "up to 5%-8%". Small groups (5–10 stores) get ~8%, and Sonic, AutoNation and Lithia are "in your 5%s" [X, #1].
- "They complain and threaten to cancel but it seems that they rarely ever do." [X, #1] Only the smallest ~5% of the base cancels.
- "CARFAX has always been, before they do a price increase, they will typically launch something new to justify it." [X, #1; also #3]

**Headroom**
- "I think we're another 12%-15%. I think that's when the cost gets pretty high." That is about 3–4 years, unless a new value-add arrives [X, #1].
- The OEM would switch only after a breach or "a 25% price increase" [X, #4].

**Flat at a large group (the newest call)**
- "I want to say it's been the same for at least the past two years that I can remember." [X, #6]
- That is a <$5,000/month bundle (Advantage + Listings + For Life). The GM puts the flat price down to an AutoNation group deal.

**Usage true-ups**
- Dealers report "Our price doubled" and "Our price tripled" [X, #2]. One appraisal-heavy dealer went from ~$2,500 to $4,800 to ~$6,800 a month.
- #2's explanation: AI agents took pulls from 100–500 to as many as 10,000 a month on uncapped legacy contracts. "Usage is starting to run away from them." [X, #2]

**Price points**
- A single rooftop pays ~$999/month for unlimited use; capped tiers start at $250 [X, #4]. Dealers spend "a couple thousand dollars a month" [X, #2].
- The consumer report costs $45–50 [X, #3], or $29.99 for one and $49.99 for five [X, #4]. The two accounts conflict.

**AutoCheck discount to CARFAX**
- #1 and #3: "AutoCheck is roughly half." [X, #1]
- #4: $799 against $999, about 20% cheaper, and "they can go much deeper" [X, #4].
- #6: about one-third of CARFAX's price, from memory.

**BIG**
- One-year contracts with monthly minimums, billed per VIN [X, #3].
- Insurers pay $0.05–0.25 per VIN; lenders pay $0.25 to "a couple of dollars" [X, #3]. The same person gave lenders $0.50–1.00 in #1.
- Increases come every ~18 months at 3–5%. Insurance was moving to 5–8% "Once these insurance companies were a little bit more, not stable, but they have been with CARFAX more than a year or two." [X, #3]

### Category dynamics
- **Who buys what.** Dealers pay a flat monthly fee; BIG buys report fields à la carte [X, #3]. Fleet companies use the cheaper AutoCheck, mainly for recall checks [X, #4].
- **Recalls.**
  - CARFAX aggregates OEM recall data but, as a concession to OEMs, shows open recalls only [X, #5].
  - Service lanes run CARFAX to find recalls, because "recalls are easy money" for the dealer [X, #4].
- **Auctions were ceded.** Dealers paid "several dollars" a record and auctions wanted "a nickel or a dime", so CARFAX chose dealers. AutoCheck formed in the vacuum [X, #5].

### Competition

**AutoCheck**
- It has the same commodity DMV title, brand and total-loss data [X, #3], but little service, odometer or accident data [X, #1, #3].
- Experian uses it as a credit add-on: "Not a huge focus." [X, #3]
- Smaller groups and independents use AutoCheck for cost, or use both [X, #1, #3]. At #6's earlier stores AutoCheck "never lasted long" [X, #6].
- **#3 contradicts himself.** He says AutoCheck "is gaining some market share there, and it is mainly due, from my understanding, some dealers are starting to leave because of the price increases". He also says "I don't see them losing any market share in the next five years." He has no hard numbers.
- **Mazda** is the only OEM CPO program on AutoCheck. "There is precedent with Mazda already using AutoCheck, so there is a clear path for us to divest." [X, #4]

**Cox / Autotrader**
- #2 describes the switch as AutoCheck-only on Autotrader, a rejection driven by CARFAX Listings competing with Autotrader. Core CARFAX revenue was likely unaffected because of a reciprocal deal. "I think it was a little more of like an industry statement, if that makes sense." [X, #2]
- vAuto, used by ~75% of franchise dealers, still embeds CARFAX, and Cox "haven't broken it on the vAuto side yet" [X, #2].
- #3: "I don't think it's extremely important."
- #3 dates the marketplace exits to the Listings launch ("six or seven years ago"). The archive's dated sources (2013–14 and 2025–26) stand.

**Auctions and Manheim**
- AutoCheck has exclusive dealer-consigned auction data (~50% of auction volume). CARFAX gets 70–80% of fleet, rental and lease condition data straight from consignors [X, #5].
- "Manheim has a deep loyalty and relationship. They actually helped found AutoCheck." [X, #5] Manheim once demanded 25% of CARFAX in exchange for its data. Both claims are unverified.

**Others**
- NMVTIS resellers have title data only [X, #5].
- Black Book, ALG and Experian's vehicle score already compete in predictive scores and residuals [X, #1].
- Polk: users know of no registration alternative; J.D. Power PIN is the closest [X, #4]. Polk is the "de facto pathway", though it lags ~90 days [X, #2].

### The moat as rivals and customers see it
- **Police data.**
  - CARFAX reaches ~70% of crashes [X, #1], or 70–75% of police municipalities [X, #3].
  - It gets the data through a crash-report digitisation program: agencies earn $5–10 fees and CARFAX gets the report at once [X, #5].
- **Service data.**
  - 90,000+ shops, with >90% coverage [X, #1, #3].
  - Service and accident data are "roughly 60%" of the database that AutoCheck lacks [X, #1].
  - Odometer readings are the key advantage in insurance [X, #1, #3, #5].
- **Database growth.** About 30k sources and 15bn records in 2013, rising to 150k+ and ~40bn [X, #3]. The archive has 177k and 38bn [D].
- **Exclusivity is partial.**
  - "My best guess would be 20% would be unique, 80% would be common." That is #5 on collision data.
  - On buying from estimatics vendors: "We were held hostage by these companies." [X, #5]
  - On shops and police: "There's nothing exclusive about it." [X, #3]
- **Replicable, but not worth it.**
  - #1: Experian costed a rebuild at 3–5 years for 20–25% share and saw no ROI. An entrant has no give-get to offer: "They don't have any data that a shop or a dealer would necessarily want." [X, #1]
  - #3: 5–10 years for 30–40% share.
- **Economics.** "The margins in the vehicle history data business are crazy because in most cases, you own the data, you buy the data." [X, #5]
- **Weak spot.** The VHR struggles with accident severity: "both of these companies struggle with accident severity in their reports" [X, #2]. ADAS repairs get graded as minor.

### Channel

**OEM CPO**
- Every OEM except Mazda mandates CARFAX for certified cars [X, #4].
- Nissan (1,176 dealers) and Infiniti (292) run a CARFAX report twice on every CPO car and use the three-year buyback guarantee [X, #4].
- The OEM's own bill is only a couple of thousand dollars a quarter for a region of ~280 dealers [X, #4].

**CARFAX Listings at #6's store**
- It is the **#1 used-car lead source by volume**: 78 leads this month, against CarGurus 60, Cars.com 43, Autotrader 37, TrueCar 16 and Edmunds 15.
- Its year-to-date close rate is one of the lowest [X, #6]:

| Source | YTD close rate |
|---|---|
| CarGurus | 4.54% |
| Autotrader | 4.29% |
| Cars.com | 3.55% |
| CARFAX Listings | 1.98% |
| Edmunds | 1.88% |
| TrueCar | 1.08% |

- "It's the highest volume of leads, but it's one of the lowest, if not the lowest, closing percentage of leads." [X, #6] He suspects shoppers bounce to CARFAX to read the free report.

**The marketplace switch, seen from the dealer**
- "Transparently, I really wasn't aware that most of the current sites would be showing that." [X, #6]
- "No, none of our vendors have brought it up recently." That was his answer when asked whether anyone had pitched AutoCheck.

**Integrations and marketplace norms**
- CARFAX sits in vAuto [X, #2] and in shop POS systems: "It also has built-in extensions with ALLDATA, CARFAX, and other systems like that we can go in and use and pull information from." [X, #8]
- Marketplaces sell on 30-day outs with 20–40% discounts [X, #7]; "most of the providers have a 90-day out" [X, #6].

### Insurance, lending and new data products
- **Sizing.** BIG is "less than 40%" of ~$1bn [X, #3].
  - "I'd say a majority of BIG's revenue is coming from insurance, and then less than 20% is auto finance." [X, #3]
  - Insurance is expected "to almost double in the next five years" [X, #3].
- **Insurance onboarding.**
  - About half of insurers bought data by 2024 [X, #1].
  - Onboarding takes 2–3 years plus state approvals: "It was taking CARFAX two to three years to onboard a new insurance company from the time they said they were interested." [X, #1]
  - Adoption is projected above 90% by 2030 [X, #3].
- **Lending is a commodity.** About 90% of lenders pull vehicle history; the rest are prime lenders who pull none. The CARFAX/AutoCheck split is ~50/50, and subprime lenders often buy both [X, #1, #3].
- **Pipeline products** [X, #1, #3]:
  - a lien-holder data exchange (started 2023);
  - "reliability and repair" predictive data;
  - Car Care white-labelled inside dealer and insurer apps ("They charge them for it. It's big bucks.").
- **Dealer view of For Life.** "The truth is I don't really know if it's added much in terms of business with that." It would be the first item cut [X, #6].

### International
- "I think it's always going to be a sideshow, with the idea it could grow." [X, #1] European data is more restricted. No expert sizes it.

### AI
- **Upside.** "AI could be the best thing possible for them, because it enables people to use their product more." [X, #2] He advises usage tiers, blocking model-training aggregation, and selling "data through API".
- **Risk.** If Experian offered the same source data at half price: "Fine, I don't really care where it comes from." [X, #2]
  - Dealers are pressed to cut tools in favour of AI. CARFAX "might be one of the last things to go" [X, #2].
- **Not covered.** No expert addressed AI answer engines diverting consumer traffic.

### Management and culture
- **Bill Eager.** "Bill was a very good operator, in my opinion." He is analytical and drives price increases, but is not inspirational [X, #1, #3]. #5, his friend, rates him "a 9.5"; the blind spot is unpopular lieutenants.
- **Culture.** "It felt like it turned more corporate when Bill took over, and it was like you had less access to managers above you than you did before." [X, #1] He attributes this to growth to 1,000+ staff and to the return to office.
- **Silos.** "I don't think those discussions ever took place." That is #5 on forcing integration across CARFAX, Polk and aM. Field sales were protective of their territory.
  - "A lot of the CARFAX product owners, etc., they don't come from the automotive world." [X, #5]
  - #6 still deals with separate aM and CARFAX reps.

## 4. Challenges and new risks the archive lacked

1. **Usage repricing backlash.** AI and automated appraisals multiply pulls on legacy contracts, and the true-ups show up as doubled or tripled bills [X, #2]. That is upside if priced well, and a churn trigger toward a half-price AutoCheck if not.
2. **Price on the churn-prone cohort.** Large groups pay 4–5% increases or are flat [X, #1, #6]. Small stores pay ~8% and are the ones that cancel [X, #1].
3. **The marketplace switch costs Listings, not the VHR.** Listings leads may be report-seekers diverted from the marketplaces, closing at about half the marketplace rate. Listings is the first thing a GM would cut [X, #6]. It is ~22% of CARFAX revenue.
4. **vAuto dependency.** About 75% of franchise dealers appraise inside Cox's vAuto with CARFAX embedded [X, #2]. A break there would cost more than Autotrader did.
5. **Accident-severity accuracy.** Both providers misgrade ADAS-era damage [X, #2]. That is a trust and buyback-guarantee exposure [X, #4].
