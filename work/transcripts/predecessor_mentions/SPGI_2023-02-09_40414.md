## Event summary
- Mobility (reported as a division) grew revenue 9% in Q4 2022, led by Dealer (+9%, CARFAX subscriptions), Manufacturing (+8%, Polk Automotive Solutions and recall deals) and Financials and other (+10%, insurance underwriting). Expenses rose 15% (headcount, ad timing, cloud), cutting operating profit 2% and margin by 380 bps; TTM margin 39%.
- 2023 guidance for Mobility: revenue +6.5% to +8.5%, margin 39%-40%, driven by auto supply-chain normalization, price realization, new business and new product adoption; expense growth expected to moderate quickly and be below revenue growth.
- On Q&A, management said Mobility is resilient across used, new and EV markets: OEM/dealer margin pressure from normalization could hit retention, offset by higher demand for marketing and sales products; confident in the 6.5%-8.5% range.
- CARFAX, automotiveMastermind and Polk are named as the product set serving OEMs, suppliers, dealers, insurers and lenders.
- Mobility's e-fees revenue is included in the new "sustainability and energy transition" revenue base ($247M in 2022 versus $209M under old ESG definition).
- A portfolio-review comment (Engineering Solutions sale to KKR already announced) was made in a Mobility-related question; no Mobility separation was announced.

### 4. Doug Peterson, President and CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Stephanie Moore, Jefferies): how will Mobility fare in 2023 given falling used-car prices and rising new-vehicle production; CARFAX seems resilient but could benefit; other drivers?
- Quote: "All of this has driven all of these different types of players to the mobility business for data, for analytics, for research, for forecasting."
- Detail: Peterson describes Mobility as a set of capabilities starting with CARFAX, plus automotiveMastermind and Polk Analytics, serving OEMs, suppliers, dealers, insurers and automotive lenders. Volatility over the last three years has driven an incredible digital transformation and pushed these players toward the business for data, analytics, research and forecasting. EV transition adds another demand driver. The business can benefit in used, new and dealer-incentive environments, so it is very resilient; S&P uses its own data to forecast its own business.
