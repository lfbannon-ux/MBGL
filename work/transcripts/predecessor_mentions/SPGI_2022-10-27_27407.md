## Event summary
- Mobility was a clear bright spot in Q3 2022: revenue +8% y/y, adjusted operating profit +14%, margin +200 bps, TTM adjusted margin 40%, 78% recurring revenue.
- Growth was driven by CARFAX new business and high retention; dealer revenue +10% on strong CARFAX demand because dealership profitability stayed high. Marketing products powered by Mobility remained muted.
- Management explained an "inherent hedge" in the business: used-car/CARFAX strength offsets weaker sales and marketing (OEM/dealer) products if the car market normalises (new-car inventories rise).
- Peterson named three trends watched in automotive: used-car market, OEM and new vehicle supply chain recovering from historic lows, and the EV transition. All seen as favourable to Mobility data and analytics.
- Management said Mobility is a "phenomenal business" with strong innovation and launches, and they expect continued good performance. No spin or separation discussion; the context was the 8-month-old IHS Markit merger.

### 4. Doug Peterson, President and CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Ashish Sabadra): Mobility momentum is strong despite weakness in auto lending/demand; as auto demand slows, how should growth be thought of and is the business counter-cyclical?
- Quote: "As the market returns to new autos coming into the market and inventories rise, that we will benefit from products and services to the OEMs as well as the dealers are gonna start having a different type of relationship again with the manufacturers and suppliers."
- Detail: Peterson said there has been an interesting shift in the last couple of years. When the market slowed from the pandemic and supply-chain disruption, the used-auto market expanded and Mobility benefited from the CARFAX suite, especially at dealers. As new autos return and inventories rise, Mobility will benefit through products and services for OEMs, and dealers will rebuild a different relationship with manufacturers and suppliers. He said management was watching three big trends in automotive (continued in next entry).

### 7. Ewout Steenbergen, EVP and CFO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Jeff Silber, BMO): what green shoots to look for in the ratings market; the answer closed with a statement that the company is always aspiring to improve margins across all businesses, which led into Mobility.
- Quote: "You could say there's a kind of an inherent hedge in that business."
- Detail: Mobility is not an exception to the aim of improving margins. The environment is supportive, particularly with low inventory levels. OEM and dealer margins are high, driving higher sales and marketing product demand because inventory is low. If supply and demand in the car market shift, the different revenue streams (sales/marketing vs used-car/CARFAX) would move in opposite directions. Management therefore sees itself as insulated financially and able to keep focusing on margin expansion.
