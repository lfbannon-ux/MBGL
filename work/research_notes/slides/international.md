## 2. Lay explanation (for a 15-year-old)

CARFAX already tells Americans the hidden history of a used car. CARFAX International does the same in other countries, starting with Canada and a handful of European countries. Each country keeps its car records differently (and in Europe privacy rules are strict), so CARFAX has to sign up local data sources country by country, then sell reports to dealers and shoppers there.

- A family in Toronto buying a 2018 Toyota RAV4 gets a CARFAX Canada report showing it was registered in Ontario, had a minor accident in 2021 and still has a loan (lien) owed on it, so they make the seller pay off the loan first.
- A car imported from the US into Canada: the Canadian report also pulls in its US history, so a flood-damaged car from Florida can't be "washed" by crossing the border.
- A German shopper on mobile.de clicks a CARFAX report link on a used VW Golf and sees the mileage recorded at earlier inspections, catching a rolled-back odometer.

## 3. How it makes money

- **Same model as the U.S.:** dealer subscriptions plus pay-per-report consumer and transactional sales. Filing: "Internationally, CARFAX leads with a consumer-first strategy (e.g., in Spain, Italy, Poland and expansion in Canada)." [D, Form 10/A p.380]
- **Canada is more transactional than the U.S.:** "The impact was concentrated in our B2B business and in CARFAX Canada, where volume-linked transactional revenue represents a larger share of the mix than the rest of CARFAX." [IR, Eager, Q2-26, 7-Aug-2026, [243.34s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4111696&documentType=transcript&eventId=704668&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=243.34)]
- **Canada product suite incomplete:** "We have a very large, well-established business in Canada that has a couple of our products, but not the full product suite." [IR, Calderone, GS, [457.2s](https://web.quartr.com/companies/22709?companyId=22709&documentId=4152674&documentType=transcript&eventId=725748&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&targetTime=457.2)]
- **Germany:** CARFAX Europe bills dealers directly; mobile.de is a sales partner (reports shared by link/PDF in the dealer area); launch promo 25% off (code MOBILE25) [3P Autohaus; mobile.de].
- **Price points:** none disclosed or stated by experts for international.

## 4. Customers

- **Canada:** dealers, auctions (historically Manheim/ADESA), OEM certified and off-lease programs, lenders (lien check), consumers; integrated in Kijiji Autos listings [3P].
- **Europe:** consumers first ("consumer-first strategy" [D 10/A p.380]); dealers via marketplaces (mobile.de in Germany).
- **Car Care users:** 53M+ in "the U.S. and Canada" [IR, Eager, Investor Day 699.33s] (no Canada split).
- **Addressable fleet (deck slide 75, "massive whitespace"; region-to-value mapping inferred from print order):** US ~292M, Canada ~28M, EMEA ~506M, APAC ~612M vehicles in operation [D-deck].
- **Core TAM** includes "US CA IT ES PL" (Vehicle History, Car Listings, Car Care, Banking & Insurance): $8bn, CARFAX "share of core" 15%; extended TAM incl. "Internationalization" $18bn [D-deck slide 71; third-party consulting estimates].
- No customer counts disclosed by country.

## 6. Moat (plain words)

- **Canada:** CARFAX bought the incumbent and its institutional channels (dealer associations, auctions, OEM programs) and now claims market leadership [IR]. Moat = incumbency + channel, not proven by any disclosed share figure.
- **Europe:** no moat yet; the bet is that CARFAX's "formula" travels: "go acquire the best information, combine it with our trusted brand, and then connect it to the dealers and the consumers there." [IR, Fredericks, Investor Day 10394.4s]. Data access is the gating factor (Germany opened when "the VIN is no longer considered PII" [IR]).
- **Shared platform:** Europe now runs on the CARFAX U.S. ingestion platform, which pulled Germany forward ~6 months [IR].
- **Counter-evidence:** "It's my understanding that the data over there, it's a lot more restricted than it is in the States." [X #1]; local free government data (Poland CEPIK, UK MOT) lowers willingness to pay [3P].
