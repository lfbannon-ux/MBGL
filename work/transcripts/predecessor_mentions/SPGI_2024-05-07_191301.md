## Event summary

- Peterson names the mobility business as one of five portfolio pillars (Ratings, Market Intelligence, Commodity Insights, Mobility, Indices).
- He disclosed that a tiny business inside Mobility was sold the year before, too small to have been discussed, as part of continuous portfolio pruning.
- Mobility outlook: unique, publicly-unique data and analytics asset (DMV, consumer and dealer data); COVID shifted OEM-dealer relationships and made dealers data-driven ("Moneyball"); MarketScan acquisition added pricing data; industry transformation (EV, autonomous, supply chain) a tailwind.
- Mobility was one of the smaller contributors to the IHS Markit synergy targets (targets 80M cost, achieved 20M; 50M revenue, 80M realized).
- No numeric Mobility results or guidance given.

## Entries

### 1. Doug Peterson (President and CEO) - Q&A
- Question (Patnaik): what surprises, upside and downside, came from the IHS Markit merger and how does the combined portfolio look.
- Quote: "we've got the ratings franchise, we've got the Market Intelligence, we've got an incredibly powerful Commodity Insights franchise, the mobility business, and then finally, the index business."
- Detail: Net-net the merger was very successful (strong team, brand, execution). Downside surprise: some IHS Markit businesses were more market-driven than expected (e.g., the Enterprise Solutions business hit by slow M&A). Portfolio now has five franchises including mobility; synergies across the portfolio are an upside, especially energy transition and sustainability. Context: cost synergies target $480M achieved $620M; revenue synergy target $350M within about 2.5 years, $180M already realized, largely cross-sell.

### 3. Doug Peterson (President and CEO) - Q&A
- Question (Patnaik): shift to Mobility, the last segment, which has surprised new investors with how well it has grown; what is the outlook.
- Quote: "Well, the Mobility business is another one that's fairly unique. There's not another publicly traded company that has a business like this inside of it, this deep data and analytics."
- Detail (turns at 2102, 2145, 2185): Data extracted similarly to the rest of S&P Global: publicly available data from DMVs, plus consumer- and dealer-provided data. Business growing quickly, partly through big wins from COVID, which changed the OEM-dealer relationship; dealers realized (Moneyball analogy) they could use data and analytics to run their business and predict buyers. S&P built an ecosystem of manufacturers, supply chain, dealers and auto financiers, and applied discipline in running and investing. Acquired MarketScan, which brings unique pricing information. Industry undergoing massive transformation (EVs, autonomous, supply chain), for which Mobility has needed solutions: "great timing". No numbers given.
