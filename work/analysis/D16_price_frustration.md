# D16: Customer frustration with CARFAX price increases (2026-10-27)

**Sources:**
- 9 AlphaSense expert calls (8 people; #1 and #3 are the same person) [X]. Quotes are verbatim from the transcripts.
- Transcripts [IR].
- Search-result snippets on dealer forums and the 2013+ litigation [context]. Full pages were not retrievable.

## Bottom line

- **Frustration is loud but rarely acted on.** "They complain and threaten to cancel but it seems that they rarely ever do." [X #1, ex-CARFAX, Jul 2026]
- **Who complains most:**
  - Small and independent stores. They pay ≈8% rises and they are the ones who cancel.
  - Heavy, appraisal-driven or automated users whose usage is being re-tiered (bills doubling or tripling).
- **Large groups** negotiate flat or 4–5% rises.
- **Insurers and lenders** pay cents per VIN.
- **Direction: rising.** Usage true-ups in 2025–26, AutoCheck picking up price-driven defectors, rival marketplace reps now pitching AutoCheck, and a "modest" retention decline already flagged in 2023.

## Evidence by customer group

| Group | Frustration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Small and independent dealers | **High; acts on it** | "The only ones that we ever saw cancel, at least during my time, were the smaller ones." [X #1]. Increases look like 8–10% to them [X #1]. "It is most expensive for a one-rooftop store." [X #4] |
| Heavy, appraisal or automated users | **High (new)** | "Our price doubled," "Our price tripled," I talked to a dealer about that two weeks ago." [X #2, ex-Cox AVP, Jul 2026]. One dealer: ≈$2,500 → $4,800 → ≈$6,800/month [X #2] |
| Franchised dealers generally | **Medium: complaints, little switching** | "The biggest complaint you get from CARFAX is that, "Hey, man, it is expensive," and they are really the only game in town." [X #4, Nissan/ex-CARFAX rep]. "Things that I've heard from dealers directly on CARFAX in the last couple of years, one, they've gotten too expensive." [X #2] |
| Large groups (AutoNation) | **Low today, conditional** | "We haven't had a price increase." [X #6, AutoNation GM, 30 Sep 2026]. Open to AutoCheck "if there is a significant cost saving" (AlphaSense summary). The first cut would be For Life, not the core [X #6] |
| OEMs | **Low** | Would switch only after something negative or "a 25% price increase" [X #4] |
| Insurers | **Low so far, increases accelerating** | 3–5% rises, "pretty aggressive, in the 5%-8% range" for insurance; "The reason they can increase price fairly aggressively is because there's not a huge competitor out there." [X #3] |
| Lenders | **Price-sensitive (commodity)** | ≈50/50 CARFAX/AutoCheck split; subprime lenders buy both [X #1, #3] |

## Is frustration turning into churn?

- **Some, at the small end:** "It does seem like AutoCheck is gaining some market share there, and it is mainly due, from my understanding, some dealers are starting to leave because of the price increases. It's just getting too costly for them." [X #3]
  - The same expert also says he doesn't see CARFAX losing share in five years [X #3].
  - Insider economics: the price lift "was so much greater than even losing the bottom 5% of the business" [X #1].
- **Rivals are now pushing churn:** the interviewer's premise, which the GM didn't confirm, was that Autotrader and CarGurus reps are "now incentivized to get dealers to switch to AutoCheck" [X #6]. Kelley Blue Book and Autotrader co-branded AutoCheck in June 2025 [challengers report].
- **The brake on churn is consumer demand:** switching to AutoCheck could save "about two-thirds" (AlphaSense summary of #6), but shoppers ask for CARFAX: "we end up having to try to source a CARFAX for someone" [X #6].
- **Company record:**
  - "retention rates in Mobility have declined modestly" (S&P Global, Q1-2023) [IR].
  - The NRR number was removed from the Investor Day slides; only "meaningfully north of 100%" was given [IR].
  - No churn or retention figure has ever been disclosed.

## History: frustration has boiled over before

- **2012–13:** dealer forums record steep jumps: a flat rate moving to $1,899/month (2012), and a bill going from $462 to $1,980 tied to report volume [context, DealerRefresh snippets, ≈2012–13].
- **April 2013:** ≈120 dealers sued, growing to ≈469 in the 10-K count. Trade headlines cite 530 and 700+, alleging dealers were forced to pay inflated prices through exclusive deals [context]. CARFAX won summary judgment in 2016, affirmed in 2018 [10-K / secondary].
- **A separate suit (date not confirmed):** Law360 headline: "Used Car Dealer Says CarFax Arbitrarily Hikes Monthly Fees" [context].
- **Inference:** the 2012–13 episode was a volume/usage re-pricing, the same pattern as the 2025–26 usage true-ups [X #2]. Last time it produced litigation but not mass defection.

## How management manages it [X]

- **New features before each increase:** "before they do a price increase, they will typically launch something new to justify it." [X #1]
- **Negotiated rises:** "maybe they go out with an 8%, but the dealers say, "Hey, that is too much," then they negotiate it down" [X #1].
- **Group deals:** flat pricing for groups such as AutoNation [X #6].
- **Bundling:** the Lifetime bundle (Q4-25) lengthened sales cycles and was reversed in June 2026 [IR, Q2-26 call].
