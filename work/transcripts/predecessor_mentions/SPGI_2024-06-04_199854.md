## Event summary
- The only Mobility segment of the chat was a direct question from the host on Mobility's growth runway and fit within S&P. No financial numbers (growth rate, margin, guidance) were given for Mobility.
- Peterson frames Mobility as a data and analytics business serving an industry undergoing a massive shift (ICE to electric, hybrid, potentially hydrogen) and serving OEMs, dealers, recall management, CARFAX used-car information, and insurers/lenders.
- Runway argument: the business kept growing through the pandemic chip shortage because the used-car shift increased demand for its data; he sees ample room to grow as long as the industry is transforming and people keep buying cars.
- The MarketScan acquisition adds vehicle pricing and market-value data, which he says completes the supply-chain data set and is difficult for any competitor to match (Rosenbaum names Experian in the UK).
- On fit: Mobility benefits from S&P technology, AI and data-extraction tools; Peterson notes some end customers differ and says the company is "always evaluating the total portfolio, especially at the product level", but calls Mobility a really good business, with no separation or spin announced.

## Entries

### 1. Doug Peterson, CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Shlomo Rosenbaum, Stifel): Mobility was IHS Markit's fastest-growing business; how long can the growth last and how does it fit within S&P?
- Quote: "It's exciting to be in a data and analytics business when you're serving an industry that is shifting from ICE, internal combustion engine, systems to electric, potentially hydrogen, to hybrid."
- Detail: Automotive and mobility globally are going through a massive transformation, creating doubt and uncertainty across the supply chain down to consumers; S&P supplies data and analytics for informed decisions. Products serve manufacturers; dealers and dealer groups working with OEMs on inventory management, rebates and interest-rate subsidies; and recall management for the industry. CARFAX provides individual used-car information (value arises "one second after it leaves" the lot) used for buying and selling, plus aggregate statistics. A less-discussed client set is insurers and financial institutions using the data for underwriting and lending. He describes a very broad customer set and an industry that has been growing quickly, and says Mobility has benefited from S&P Global technology, AI and data-extraction tools.

### 3. Doug Peterson, CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question (Rosenbaum): is there enough runway for Mobility's growth to continue?
- Quote: "The used car market completely took off."
- Detail: During the pandemic, chip and parts shortages made vehicle manufacturing a struggle; the used-car market took off, and the Mobility business kept growing because it could supply information to manufacturers, lenders, insurers and dealers for that shift, and "benefited tremendously". He concludes that as long as the industry is transforming and people keep buying cars, there is a lot of room to grow.

### 4. Doug Peterson, CEO - Q&A
- Analyst question: same (Rosenbaum), continued; Peterson adds the MarketScan acquisition.
- Quote: "We've made an acquisition in the business of a company called Market Scan, and this is a specialized business for the automotive sector that has the best information about value of cars, pricing and value of cars."
- Detail: Before MarketScan, Mobility had data on which cars would be in demand, what to build and when buyers would buy, but not what they would pay or market value. MarketScan fills that gap, giving "a very complete set of information and data" across the supply chain. He asks if any other company has this combination; Rosenbaum answers that Experian in the UK is a partial competitor and that software and insurance firms do some valuation, but nobody has the whole picture. Peterson calls it a unique, valuable asset that is hard to recreate.
