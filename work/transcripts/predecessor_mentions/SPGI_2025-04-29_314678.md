## Event summary

- S&P Global announced its intent to spin Mobility into a standalone public company: tax-free, expected to complete in 12-18 months, subject to market conditions and customary regulatory approvals; Investor Day set for Nov 13 in New York with a refreshed multi-year strategy for the four core divisions.
- Mobility scale: 2024 revenue of $1.6 billion at an adjusted operating margin of nearly 40%, versus nearly $13 billion revenue and about 50% margin for the four core divisions. More than 70% of Mobility revenue is tied to the used car market and more than 80% is subscription.
- Q1 2025 Mobility results: revenue +9% (about 70 bps FX headwind), dealer +11% (CARFAX, automotiveMastermind), manufacturing +1% (recall-transaction decline, lapping from Q2), financials and other +11%; segment margin 38.5% (+40 bps). Full-year Mobility revenue guidance unchanged.
- CARFAX: Car Care has about 46 million users (nearly +65% since the 2022 investor day), backed by more than 35 billion vehicle history records.
- Tariffs: management expects some budget pressure with OEM clients but says subscription structure and the used-car mix insulate the unit; the used market may benefit from higher new-car prices.
- Rationale and carve-out: management framed the spin as best path to long-term shareholder value and greater focus; initial stranded/dis-synergy costs look immaterial to S&P Global; more detail on capital structure and licensing later in 2025-26.

### 16. Auto tariffs sensitivity
- Speaker/section: Martina Cheung, President and CEO; Q&A (Jason Haas, Wells Fargo, asked about Mobility's sensitivity to auto tariffs, any impact so far, customer conversations)
- Quote: "However, recall that this is largely on a subscription basis, and there we are reasonably insulated from those impacts."
- Detail: Management expects tariff-driven reduced imports and potentially lower manufacturing (imported components), which could pressure existing inventories and raise prices, and anticipates possible budget pressure in dialogue with OEM manufacturing clients. The OEM business is largely subscription so reasonably insulated, and S&P is helping manufacturers navigate. The business is about 70% used-car related, and the used market could benefit from higher new-car prices and lower new inventory. Vehicle history, pricing and incentive/sales and marketing data are critical to dealers in this environment. Some pressure on end customers but no direct impact yet, which is a reason for confidence in the full-year guide (3883s continuation).
